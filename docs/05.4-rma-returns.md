## 1. Purpose

Returns are where a wholesale system's commercial rules meet its inventory rules, and where both get tested by an unhappy customer.

The business already has rules: 14-day window, a restocking charge of **20% or £25, whichever is greater**, and exclusions for consumables, hygiene items and opened electricals. Today those rules live in prose on a terms page and are applied — or argued about — by hand.

This module makes them computed, enforced at request time, and auditable.

**Those are trade rules.** An order with no company — a public customer's or a guest's (05.15) — is a consumer contract. The law gives consumers a right to cancel that no fee, exclusion list or window from dispatch may reduce, and a deadline for the refund. §13 sets the consumer rules. Where §13 differs from §4–§9, §13 wins for those orders.

---

## 2. What the reference system gets wrong

| Reference system | Consequence | Requirement here |
|---|---|---|
| Rules stated in prose only | Applied inconsistently; disputed per case | Computed from data, identically every time |
| Restocking fee calculated manually | Arithmetic errors, and always in someone's favour | `max(20% of line value, £25)` computed per §6 |
| Exclusions discovered during the dispute | Customer ships back goods that were never returnable | `is_refundable` enforced at request, before shipping |
| No RMA number | Goods arrive at the warehouse unattributable | Mandatory RMA reference, required on the parcel |
| Returns go straight back to stock | Damaged and expired goods re-sold | Inspection state between receipt and restock |
| No batch attribution on inbound | Returned goods lose their batch; recall trace broken | Batch recovered from the dispatch trace, or quarantined |
| No credit note trail | Refunds reconciled by hand against invoices | `credit_notes` reducing credit exposure automatically |

## 3. User stories

| # | As a… | I want to… | So that… |
|---|---|---|---|
| U1 | trade buyer | request a return from my order history | I don't have to telephone |
| U2 | trade buyer | be told immediately what is returnable and what it will cost me | I can decide before paying carriage |
| U3 | trade buyer | receive an RMA number and instructions | my parcel is identifiable on arrival |
| U4 | trade buyer | track the return to refund | I'm not chasing |
| U5 | warehouse operative | see expected returns and book them in against the RMA | goods aren't stranded on the dock |
| U6 | warehouse operative | inspect and route goods to restock, quarantine or write-off | damaged stock isn't re-sold |
| U7 | accounts staff | have credit notes generated with the fee applied | I'm not recomputing by hand |
| U8 | administrator | waive a fee with a reason | goodwill is possible and recorded |
| U9 | administrator | see return rate by SKU and reason | I can drop a line that keeps coming back |

## 4. State machine

```
requested ──▶ approved ──▶ awaiting_goods ──▶ received ──▶ inspected ──▶ resolved
    │             │                               │            │
    └──▶ rejected └──▶ cancelled                  │            └──▶ partially_resolved
                                                  └──▶ not_received (expired)
```

`rmas.status`. Per-line outcomes are independent, which is why `partially_resolved` exists: three lines returned, two restocked and refunded, one written off with no refund, is a normal outcome.

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `requested` | Customer submitted; eligibility already applied |
| `approved` | RMA number issued, instructions sent |
| `rejected` | Declined with a reason |
| `awaiting_goods` | Approved, return window open |
| `received` | Parcel booked in at the warehouse |
| `inspected` | Every line dispositioned |
| `resolved` | Credit note or replacement issued; complete |
| `partially_resolved` | Mixed line outcomes, all settled |
| `not_received` | Approved but nothing arrived before the deadline |
| `cancelled` | Customer withdrew |

### 5.1 `rmas`

```sql
CREATE TABLE rmas (

    company_id IS NULL AND cancellation_notified_at IS NOT NULL

  WHERE status = 'awaiting_goods';

CREATE INDEX rmas_reason_idx ON rmas (return_reason, requested_at);
-- §13.5: the staff alert 3 days before a consumer refund falls due

    AND status IN ('approved','awaiting_goods','received','inspected');

- `rmas_waiver_chk` makes an unexplained, unattributed fee waiver structurally impossible. Waiving a £25 charge is a commercial concession and must carry a name.
- `rmas_return_by_idx (status, return_by_date)` drives the sweep that closes approved RMAs where nothing arrived.
- `rmas_reason_idx (return_reason, requested_at)` powers the return-rate-by-reason report — the data that tells you a SKU is a problem rather than a customer being difficult.
- **Consumer rows (§13, signed off 2026-10-04).** `company_id IS NULL` means a consumer order, and the RMA reaches its customer through `order_id` (`NOT NULL`), as payments do (02 §19). `rmas_consumer_fee_chk` makes a restocking fee or carriage recharge on a consumer return impossible to persist. `rmas_cancellation_chk` makes a statutory cancellation without its notification time and possession day impossible. The delivery refund columns are consumer-only; the cap at the standard delivery charge (§13.5) reads `orders.standard_shipping_net_minor`, which a `CHECK` cannot, so the service enforces it and the tests assert it.

  -- a batch-tracked line cannot be restocked without a recovered batch (§7.4)

    CHECK (disposition <> 'restock' OR restocked_base_qty = 0 OR batch_id IS NOT NULL
           OR quarantined_base_qty >= 0),

```

- `uq_rl_rma_order_line` prevents the same order line appearing twice on one RMA. The cumulative guard across *all* RMAs is §6.1 rule 2.
- `rma_lines_disposed_chk` guarantees dispositions never exceed what actually arrived — the constraint that stops phantom stock entering the ledger.
- `rma_lines_received_chk` allows over-receipt up to double (customers do send extra) while catching a keying error of an order of magnitude.
- Prices are **copied from the order line, never re-resolved**. A refund is against what was charged, which may differ from today's price. Same principle as 03 §3.4.

### 5.3 `stock_serials` linkage

`rma_line_id` already exists on `stock_serials` (02 §7.6) with `stock_serials_rma_line_idx`. Returned serial-tracked units are scanned at receipt and matched against serials dispatched on the originating order line. A serial that was never dispatched on that order is rejected at the receipt screen — that is either a different customer's unit or a fraud attempt, and both need a human.

## 6. Eligibility — computed at request time

The whole point of this module. Nothing here is discovered later.

### 6.1 Rules

Applied in order; the first failure stops that line.

| # | Rule | Source | On failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Return window: `now <= order.dispatched_at + window` (default 14 days) | Config | Line not returnable; days-expired stated |
| 2 | `requested_base_qty <= dispatched_base_qty − Σ returned_base_qty` across all prior RMAs | `order_lines` | Capped at the returnable figure, shown |
| 3 | `skus.is_refundable = 1` | 02 §5.5 | **Not returnable.** The stored `non_refundable_reason` is shown verbatim — consumable, hygiene, sealed electrical or bespoke |
| 4 | Order dispatched at all | `orders.status` | Undispatched: this is a cancellation, not a return. Routed to cancellation |
| 5 | Batch not recalled | `batches.status` | Always returnable regardless of window or refundability. A recall overrides every other rule |

**For a consumer order, rules 1, 3 and 4 are replaced** by §13: the window runs from possession, not dispatch (§13.3); only the statutory exclusions apply, not `is_refundable` (§13.3); and an undispatched order is cancelled by §13.2. Rules 2 and 5 stand.

**Rule 3 is the one the reference system gets most expensively wrong.** Its exclusions are prose on a terms page, so a customer ships back £400 of opened electricals at their own cost and then argues. Here the request screen refuses the line before they pay carriage, quoting the reason held against the SKU.

**Rule 5 inverts everything.** A recalled batch is returnable outside the window, at our carriage cost, with no fee, whatever `is_refundable` says. Safety overrides commerce, and it is expressed as a rule rather than left to whoever answers the phone.

### 6.2 Restocking fee

The business rule is **20% of line value or £25, whichever is greater**, per line. **Trade only:** a consumer return never carries a restocking fee (§13.5, enforced by `rmas_consumer_fee_chk`).

```
line_fee_minor = max(
    round_half_up(line_goods_net_minor × restocking_rate_bp / 10000),
    minimum_fee_minor
)
```

Go-live defaults are `restocking_rate_bp = 2000` (20.00%) and `restocking_minimum_minor = 2500` (£25.00).

**Both are configuration, not constants.** They resolve from `system_configurations` (02 §2.7) under keys `returns.restocking_rate_bp` and `returns.restocking_minimum_minor`, scoped `company` → `location` → `global`. An administrator can therefore set a softer rate for a key account, or a different minimum at a second warehouse, from the admin UI without a deployment.

**The resolved values are snapshotted onto the RMA row** (`rmas.restocking_rate_bp`, `rmas.restocking_minimum_minor`) at request time, and the fee is computed from the snapshot thereafter. Without this, changing the rate in October would silently rewrite the fee on every historical RMA, and a customer querying a March charge could not be given a straight answer. Same principle as price snapshots on order lines (03 §3.4).

Computed **per line**, not per RMA. A single £25 minimum across a five-line return would undercharge; five separate £25 minimums on a large return would be punitive. Per line is what the terms say and what the arithmetic below produces.

Worked example — grater `LTC1179`, 3 outers at £123.84, returned as "over ordered":

| | |
|---|---|
| Line goods net | £371.52 |
| 20% | £74.30 |
| Minimum | £25.00 |
| **Fee** | **£74.30** (percentage wins) |
| Refund net | £297.22 |
| Refund VAT at 20% | £59.44 |
| **Refund gross** | **£356.66** |

And a small line — 1 inner at £11.04:

| | |
|---|---|
| 20% | £2.21 |
| Minimum | £25.00 |
| **Fee** | **£25.00** (minimum wins, and exceeds the goods value) |
| Refund net | **£0.00** — clamped, never negative |

The second case is exactly why the fee is quoted **before** the customer ships. Returning a single inner of a low-value line yields nothing, and the customer must be told that at request time, not sent an invoice for the difference.

### 6.3 When no fee applies

| Reason | Fee |
|---|---|
| `damaged`, `faulty`, `wrong_item`, `wrong_quantity`, `not_as_described`, `shipping_damage`, `expired_on_arrival` | **None.** Our error or our defect; carriage at our cost |
| `over_ordered`, `no_longer_required` | Full fee; customer pays carriage |
| Recalled batch | None; carriage at our cost |
| `other` | Fee applies by default, waivable by an administrator |

Fault-based reasons carry no fee and no carriage charge. Charging a customer a restocking fee for goods we sent broken is how accounts are lost.

### 6.4 Tax on refunds

VAT is refunded on the **net refund after the fee**, at the `tax_rate_bp` snapshotted on the original order line — not today's rate.

```
line_refund_net_minor = max(0, line_goods_net_minor − line_fee_minor)
line_refund_tax_minor = round_half_up(line_refund_net_minor × tax_rate_bp / 10000)
```

The restocking fee is itself a taxable supply, and is shown as such on the credit note rather than netted invisibly. This keeps the credit note reconcilable against the original invoice line by line.

## 7. Flows

### 7.1 Request

1. Customer opens a dispatched order, selects lines and quantities in **packs**.
2. Eligibility (§6.1) runs live per line. Ineligible lines cannot be selected and state why.
3. Reason selected per line; free text optional; photographs encouraged for damage (`attachments`).
4. **Fee and net refund quoted per line and in total, before submission.** The customer confirms having seen the figures.
5. Submit → `requested`, notify the handler.

### 7.2 Approval

Auto-approval where configured (fault-based reasons under a value threshold), manual otherwise. On approval:

```
1. rma_number from number_sequences (02 §11.3)
2. return_by_date = now + return window (default 14 days to send goods back)
3. status = awaiting_goods
4. Email: RMA number, what to return, where, whether a label is provided,
   the fee, and the deadline
```

The RMA number must be visible on the parcel. Goods arriving without one are the single biggest source of stranded stock on a returns dock.

### 7.3 Receipt

Goods **do not re-enter stock on arrival.** They enter inspection — 04 §7.3.

```
1. Warehouse scans or keys the RMA number
2. Per line: record received_base_qty (may differ from requested)
3. Serial-tracked: scan each serial; verify against serials dispatched
   on the originating order line; unmatched serials rejected
4. Batch-tracked: recover batch_id from the dispatch trace
   (shipment_line_batches on the originating line).
   Ambiguous or unrecoverable → quarantine, never guess
5. status = received
```

**No stock movement is written at receipt.** The goods are physically present but not sellable and not counted as on hand. This is deliberate: the alternative is a `return_in` at receipt followed by a corrective write-off for anything that fails inspection, which pollutes the ledger with movements that describe the system's process rather than the goods' reality.

### 7.4 Inspection

Per line, a disposition:

| Disposition | Stock effect | Refund |
|---|---|---|
| `restock` | `return_in` movement, `on_hand` += qty, at the recovered batch | Yes, less fee |
| `quarantine` | `return_in` to a non-sellable location, or the batch set to `quarantined` | Per handler decision |
| `write_off` | No `return_in`. Goods never enter stock | Per handler decision; typically yes for faulty |
| `return_to_customer` | No stock effect; goods sent back | None |

Quantities may be split across dispositions on one line: 2 outers restocked, 1 written off. `rma_lines_disposed_chk` enforces that the split never exceeds what arrived.

Restock writes the movement inside the transaction that sets the disposition, per 04 §3. Batch-tracked restock **must** carry a recovered `batch_id`; if it could not be established the line cannot be dispositioned to `restock` at all. Guessing a batch destroys the recall trace, which is the one thing batch tracking exists for.

### 7.5 Resolution

```
BEGIN;
  1. Compute per-line fees and refunds (§6.2, §6.4)
  2. Create credit_notes row + lines, referencing the original invoice
  3. credit_used_minor -= credit note gross          (05.2 §8.3)
  3a. INSERT account_credit_movements (credit_note, +gross)
      UPDATE companies.account_balance_minor += gross   (§7.5A)
  4. order_lines.returned_base_qty += restocked + written_off + quarantined
  5. rmas.status = resolved | partially_resolved
COMMIT;
AFTER COMMIT: credit note PDF emailed; refund to the original payment
              method queued if the order was prepaid
```

- **Consumer orders resolve differently (§13.6):** the credit note is against the receipt, no account balance is created (there is no company to hold one), and the refund goes back to the original payment method.
- Every credit note creates an **unapplied account balance** (§7.5A). The customer then chooses: spend it at checkout, or request payout. A card order can still be refunded to the original card, and an on-account order's credit note also reduces `credit_used_minor` — but the balance is the default resting state, because it requires no gateway call and cannot fail.
- Replacement instead of credit: a replacement order is created at zero value, linked via `replacement_order_id`, allocating stock through the standard 04 §4.2 path. It can therefore fail on availability, which the handler sees immediately.
- `order_lines.returned_base_qty` increments for **everything received**, not only restocked goods. `order_lines_return_chk CHECK (returned_base_qty <= dispatched_base_qty)` (02 §8.3) then correctly prevents a second return of the same units.

### 7.5A Account credit balance

A credit note does not merely offset an invoice. It **creates an unapplied balance** on the customer's account, which they may spend at checkout or ask to be paid out. This is the third money figure on a company, distinct from the two in 05.2:

| Figure | Meaning | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| `credit_used_minor` | Invoiced and unpaid | We are owed |
| `credit_held_minor` | Confirmed orders not yet invoiced | We are owed |
| `account_balance_minor` | Unapplied credit from credit notes | **We owe** |

It does **not** increase available credit. A customer with a £10,000 limit and a £500 balance can place £10,500 of orders, because the £500 is already their money.

#### Ledger

Append-only, mirroring `stock_movements` (04 §2.1) for the same reasons — audit trail, recoverability, and an answer to "why is this figure wrong".

```sql

  CONSTRAINT account_credit_movements_reason_chk

  FOR VALUES FROM ('2026-01-01') TO ('2027-01-01');

  PARTITION OF account_credit_movements DEFAULT;

  ON account_credit_movements (company_id, occurred_at, id)
  INCLUDE (amount_minor);

```

`amount_minor` is **signed**: positive increases the balance, negative reduces it.

| Type | Sign | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| `credit_note` | + | Credit note issued (returns, goodwill, pricing correction) |
| `applied_to_order` | − | Customer spent balance at checkout |
| `refunded_to_bank` | − | Paid out by transfer |
| `refunded_to_card` | − | Refunded to the original card |
| `adjustment` | ± | Manual correction, reason code mandatory |
| `expiry` | − | Balance lapsed, if a policy is configured |
| `reversal` | ± | Compensating entry. **The only way to correct a mistake** |

Two structural choices carried over from the stock ledger, deliberately:

- **`PRIMARY KEY (id, occurred_at)`** so range partitioning by time is later a pure `ALTER` (02 §7.4). Same retrofit designed out.
- **No foreign keys**, with integrity enforced by the single writing service and asserted by reconciliation. This table is append-only and never edited.

`companies.account_balance_minor` is a projection maintained in the same transaction and rebuildable:

```sql
SELECT COALESCE(SUM(amount_minor), 0)
FROM   account_credit_movements
WHERE  company_id = ?;
```

Reconciled hourly alongside the credit projections (05.2 §7.2). Drift is a P1 and is **never auto-corrected**.

#### Applying balance at checkout

The customer chooses whether to apply it — it is never applied silently, because a buyer may be holding the balance deliberately against a larger order.

```
Inside the checkout transaction, in the 05.2 §8.2 lock order:

  1. companies row already locked FOR UPDATE for the credit check
  2. applied = min(account_balance_minor, order_total_gross_minor)
  3. INSERT account_credit_movements (applied_to_order, −applied)
  4. UPDATE companies.account_balance_minor −= applied
  5. amount_due = order_total_gross_minor − applied
  6. Remaining amount_due follows the normal payment path:
     card, BACS, or on-account credit check against the remainder
```

**No new lock tier.** The `companies` row is already held for the credit check, so applying balance adds no deadlock surface — the single global lock order of 05.2 §8.2 stands unchanged. This is why the credit-first ordering was worth establishing.

If the balance covers the order in full, `amount_due` is zero and no payment is taken. The order is `paid` with `payment_status = 'paid'` and a zero-value payment record referencing the movement, so the audit trail is continuous.

#### Paying out

An administrator can pay a balance out by bank transfer or to the original card. Both write a negative movement with an actor. Card payout is attempted through the gateway and, on failure (expired card), falls back to leaving the balance in place with accounts notified — the money stays the customer's, visible, rather than disappearing into a failed transaction.

#### Edge cases

| Case | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Balance applied, order then cancelled | Reversal restores the balance in full |
| Balance applied, order partially returned | The return's credit note creates a **new** positive movement; the original application is not unwound |
| Balance exceeds the order | Only the order total is applied; the remainder stays |
| Customer requests payout while an order is pending payment | Payout blocked for the applied portion; the rest is payable |
| Company suspended for debt but holding a balance | Balance is applicable to prepay orders. Withholding a customer's own money to enforce a debt is not defensible |
| Two checkouts racing on one balance | Serialised by the `companies` row lock; the second sees the reduced balance |

#### Tests

`account_balance_minor` = Σ ledger movements, always · balance never negative, enforced by `companies_balance_chk` · 20 parallel checkouts against one £500 balance apply at most £500 in total · applying then cancelling restores exactly · no `UPDATE` or `DELETE` ever issued against `account_credit_movements`, asserted via query log · rebuild from ledger reproduces the projection exactly on a 50,000-movement dataset.


### 7.6 Not received

The sweep on `rmas_return_by_idx` moves approved RMAs past `return_by_date` with no receipt to `not_received`, notifying customer and handler. Reopening is a new RMA, so the record of the lapsed one stands.

*Consumer RMAs (amended 2026-10-04, §13.5):* the sweep also skips any RMA with proof of sending (`goods_sent_at` set) or that we collect (`return_method = 'collection'`). The refund is owed whether or not the parcel arrives.

## 13. Consumers — cancellation and faulty goods (amendment 2026-10-04, signed off 2026-10-04)

> **Status: signed off 2026-10-04**, with the decisions in 05.15 §12 Q4–Q13 (Q13: the return cost of a pallet consignment, 02 §27). Meets 05.15 §7.2 (cancellation) and §7.3 (faulty goods). Schema in 05.15 §9 A5–A8. Build slices in 05.15 §11 (S6a–S6e). §7 of 05.15 still needs legal review before public checkout goes live (05.15 §12 Q1).

### 13.1 Who these rules apply to

An order with `orders.company_id IS NULL`: a signed-in public customer's or a guest's (05.15 §6). The RMA copies that: `rmas.company_id IS NULL`. Everything else in this document is the trade policy and stays as it is.

What changes for a consumer, at a glance:

| | Trade (§4–§9) | Consumer (§13) |
|---|---|---|
| Who can ask | Company users | The customer, signed in or by their guest order link (05.15 §6.2), or staff on their behalf |
| Window | 14 days from dispatch, configurable | 14 days after the day of possession, fixed by law (§13.3) |
| Exclusions | `is_refundable` | Statutory exclusions only (§13.3) |
| Restocking fee | 20% or £25 | **Never** |
| Before dispatch | Order amendment (05.10) | Cancel the order here, refund in full (§13.2) |
| Return carriage | Per reason (§6.3) | Customer pays for a change of mind; we pay for faulty goods (§13.5) |
| Deduction | — | Diminished value only, by staff, with a reason (§13.5) |
| Delivery refund | — | Up to the standard delivery charge (§13.5) |
| Refund to | Account balance (§7.5A) | The original payment method (§13.6) |
| Refund deadline | — | Cancellation: §13.5; faulty goods: approval date + 14 days (§13.4) |

### 13.2 Cancelling before dispatch

A consumer may cancel while **no shipment of the order has been dispatched** (`orders.status` is `confirmed` or `picking`). This is not an RMA: there is nothing to send back.

- **Who:** the customer, from the order page ("Cancel order"), signed in (`OrderPolicy`) or through their guest link; or staff, who record a cancellation the customer made by email or phone with the time the customer told us (CCR reg. 32: any clear statement is valid).
- **Whole order only** at this stage (05.15 §12 Q6). Cancelling some items before dispatch is an order amendment (05.10, not written).
- **Transaction**, in the lock order dispatch already uses (DispatchService):
  1. `orders` FOR UPDATE. Re-check that nothing is dispatched; if a dispatch won the race, answer 409 `order_already_dispatched`, and the customer cancels by returning the goods (§13.3).
  2. `shipments` of the order FOR UPDATE; any not dispatched → `cancelled`.
  3. `stock_allocations` FOR UPDATE, ascending id; `deallocation` movements through DeallocationService, which locks `stock_levels` in the 02 §11.1 order.
  4. `orders.status = 'cancelled'`, `cancelled_at = now()`, `cancellation_fee_minor = 0`.
  5. Money, with no gateway call inside the transaction (CLAUDE.md invariant 6):
     - card authorised, not captured → the payment is marked for release; the authorisation is cancelled after commit;
     - card captured → a `payments` row `type = 'refund'`, `status = 'pending'`, `refunded_payment_id` set, for the full amount; the gateway refund runs after commit;
     - BACS not yet paid → nothing to refund;
     - BACS paid → a pending `refund` row with `gateway = 'bacs'`; accounts repay by bank transfer and record it.
  6. If a receipt was issued (05.5 §7.3), a credit note `reason = 'cancellation'` for its full gross (A8), with no account movement (no company).
- **After commit:** release or refund at the gateway; the `order.cancelled` email (05.12 addition, 05.15 §9) acknowledging the cancellation on a durable medium (reg. 32(4)).
- **Refund deadline:** 14 days after we were told (reg. 34(6)). A card refund is immediate; a BACS refund appears on the accounts alert list until it is recorded, with the alert 3 days before the deadline.
- A trade order is not cancelled through this path (05.15 §12 Q11).

### 13.3 Cancelling after dispatch — the request

The customer cancels by returning goods. The RMA has `return_reason = 'consumer_cancellation'`.

**Possession day** (05.15 §7.2), snapshotted onto the RMA as `possession_on` and `possession_basis`, so a later configuration change cannot move a deadline:

| Fulfilment | `possession_basis` | `possession_on` |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery, carrier date recorded | `carrier_delivered` | The delivered date. Not available until shipments gain a delivered state (02 §14.6 open question) |
| Delivery, otherwise | `estimated_from_dispatch` | The **last** shipment's dispatch date (UK), plus the zone's `transit_days` counted as working days (Mon–Fri), plus 1 day for bank holidays. A zone with no `transit_days` uses `returns.consumer_default_transit_days` (global config, default 5). Always rounded in the buyer's favour: the latest plausible day |
| Collection | `collected` | `collection_bookings.collected_at`, as a UK date. Arrives with collection (S5b) |

**A collection order never uses dispatch + transit.** Its possession day is the day the customer
(or someone they name) collected the goods, `collection_bookings.collected_at`, and nothing else.
Until a collection is recorded as collected, the window has not started.

For an order delivered in parts, possession is the last part (reg. 30(3)(b)). While the order is `part_dispatched`, the window has not started; see 05.15 §12 Q7 for what the customer may do meanwhile.

**Window:** the request is accepted up to the end of `possession_on + 14 days` (UK). A request before possession (goods in transit) is accepted too. The 14 days are law, not configuration.

**Eligibility** replaces §6.1 rules 1, 3 and 4:

| `skus.non_refundable_reason` | Consumer cancellation |
|---|---|
| NULL (refundable) | Allowed |
| `bespoke` | **Not cancellable** — made to the consumer's specification (reg. 28(1)(b)). Stated on the request screen |
| `hygiene` | Allowed **only if the seal is unbroken** (reg. 28(1)(e)). The request screen says so; inspection may refuse an unsealed item (§13.5) |
| `consumable`, `electrical_sealed` | Allowed. These are trade exclusions, not statutory ones (05.15 §12 Q5) |

Rule 2 (quantity already returned) and rule 5 (a recalled batch is always returnable) stand.

**Approval is automatic.** A statutory cancellation has no discretion: the request is approved at once (`rma_number` from `number_sequences`, `status = 'awaiting_goods'`), with `return_by_date = cancellation_notified_at + 14 days` (reg. 35(4)). The `rma.approved` email states the return address, the deadline, and that the customer pays the return postage for a change of mind (05.15 §7.2). For a pallet consignment it states the estimated return cost given before ordering (`orders.return_cost_estimate_gross_minor`, 02 §27), or, when there is none, that we collect the goods at our cost.

`cancellation_notified_at` is the request time online, or the time staff record the customer told us. Staff can record it on the customer's behalf.

### 13.4 Faulty goods (CRA, 05.15 §7.3)

Fault reasons (`damaged`, `faulty`, `wrong_item`, `wrong_quantity`, `not_as_described`, `shipping_damage`, `expired_on_arrival`) on a consumer order:

- **Never blocked by a window**, and `is_refundable` never applies.
- Within **30 days of possession** (CRA s.22, the short-term right to reject): a full refund with no deduction, including the outbound delivery in full. Return carriage is ours.
- After 30 days the **customer chooses** repair or replacement (CRA s.23(3)). The report accepts `customer_choice` (`repair` or `replacement`); it is mandatory outside the short-term reject period. Store it in the existing `resolution_type` and retain the original choice as JSON in `internal_note` (`customer_choice`). Staff can change the remedy only with `override_basis` (`impossible` or `disproportionate`) and a non-blank `remedy_reason`, retained in that JSON with the staff user and time.
- A refund after 30 days requires `remedy_outcome` (`failed` or `refused`) and a non-blank `remedy_reason`, recorded by accounts in the same JSON. A previously resolved repair/replacement may then be refunded once; staff must record the failed attempt or refusal, never merely choose a refund. No second credit note or refund is permitted.
- On accounts approval set `refund_due_on` to the UK approval date + 14 days (CRA s.20(15)). Receipt must preserve this deadline. The existing `returns:refund-due-alerts` command covers it, once per deadline, 3 days before it falls due.
- Not auto-approved: a handler reviews the claim, as for trade.

The storefront entry point is "Report a problem" on the order page (05.15 §7.3).

### 13.5 Receipt, inspection and the refund

- **Evidence of sending** (amended 2026-10-04). On the order page the customer can say "I've sent it back" and attach proof of postage or tracking (`attachments`, type `rma`, customer-visible). **`goods_sent_at` is the moment the customer uploads it** (reg. 34(5)(b): the consumer supplies the evidence). Staff review it:
  - accepting it needs no action and changes nothing — it never moves `goods_sent_at`;
  - invalid proof (unreadable, not a postage or tracking record, not for this return) may be **rejected**: `goods_sent_at` is cleared, `refund_due_on` is recomputed, and the customer is told why (`rma.proof_rejected`) and may upload again. A later upload sets a new `goods_sent_at`.
  - **The files are kept**, never deleted: they are evidence if the refund is disputed. The rejection is recorded in the audit log (02 §15): action `rma.proof_rejected`, family `rma_disposition`, actor the staff user, subject the RMA, `reason` the text sent to the customer, `before` exactly `{goods_sent_at, last_proof_attachment_id}` and `after` exactly `{goods_sent_at: null}`. A file whose id is at or below the latest rejection's `last_proof_attachment_id` is shown to staff as rejected. No column records it.
  - A second upload while proof stands keeps the earlier `goods_sent_at`: more evidence never makes the deadline later.
  - On a `consumer_cancellation` RMA every customer attachment is proof of sending; damage photographs belong to fault returns (§13.4).
- **Refund deadline** (reg. 34(4)–(6)), recomputed whenever its inputs change:
  - `refund_due_on` = 14 days after the **earlier** of `received_at` and `goods_sent_at` (UK dates);
  - if we collect the goods (`return_method = 'collection'`): 14 days after `cancellation_notified_at`.
  - The refund may be withheld until one of those has happened (reg. 34(5)).
  - **With proof of sending, the deadline stands even if the goods never arrive** (reg. 34(5)): the refund is owed `goods_sent_at + 14 days`, and the not-received sweep skips the RMA (§7.6). Accounts follow up the missing parcel with the carrier; the customer's refund does not wait for it.
  - Accounts are alerted 3 days before `refund_due_on` (`rmas_refund_due_idx`), once per deadline.
- **Not received** (amended 2026-10-04): only an RMA with **no proof of sending and no receipt** (`goods_sent_at` and `received_at` both NULL) and not collected by us becomes `not_received` once `return_by_date` has passed, with no refund (reg. 34(5): there is nothing that makes the refund due).
- **Late goods after settlement on proof:** warehouse receipt and inspection remain available once, retaining the settled status, refund figures, credit note, payment and returned quantities. Inspection can restock goods but cannot deduct further money or reopen settlement.
- **Receipt and inspection** follow §7.3–7.4: no stock movement at receipt, `return_in` only on `restock`. An unsealed `hygiene` item is `return_to_customer` with `disposition_reason` stating the seal was broken, and is not refunded.
- **No restocking fee, ever** (`rmas_consumer_fee_chk`). No carriage recharge: the customer pays the return postage directly for a change of mind; we pay it for faulty goods.
- **Diminished value** (reg. 34(9)): staff may deduct, per line, for handling beyond what was needed to establish the goods' nature, with a reason (`rma_lines_diminished_chk`). Never automatic, never for faulty goods.
- **Arithmetic** (03 §6, integers, half-up):
  ```
  line_refund_net_minor = max(0, line_goods_net_minor − diminished_value_minor)
  line_refund_tax_minor = round_half_up(line_refund_net_minor × tax_rate_bp / 10000)
  ```
  The restocking fee term of §6.4 is always 0 here.
- **Outbound delivery refund:**
  - Cancellation: only when **nothing of the order is kept** (every unit of every line returned and refunded). Then `delivery_refund_net_minor = orders.standard_shipping_net_minor` (02 §26.3), and `delivery_refund_tax_minor = round_half_up(that × orders.shipping_tax_rate_bp / 10000)`. An upgraded delivery is refunded at the standard charge only. This is the reading flagged for legal review (05.15 §7.2).
  - Faulty goods within 30 days: the whole `orders.shipping_net_minor` and `shipping_tax_minor`, when the whole order is rejected.
- `refund_gross_minor = Σ(line_refund_net + line_refund_tax) + delivery_refund_net + delivery_refund_tax`, exactly.

### 13.6 Resolution for a consumer

```
BEGIN;
  1. Compute line refunds and the delivery refund (§13.5)
  2. Credit note against the order's receipt: company_id NULL, reason 'return' (A8).
     No account_credit_movements row — there is no company.
  3. payments row: type 'refund', status 'pending', refunded_payment_id = the original payment,
     amount = refund_gross_minor; rmas.refund_payment_id set
  4. order_lines.returned_base_qty += received (as §7.5 step 4)
  5. rmas.status = resolved | partially_resolved
COMMIT;
AFTER COMMIT: card → gateway refund to the same card (CCR reg. 34(7): the same means of
              payment); success → refund row captured, original payment refunded or
              part_refunded, orders.payment_status likewise.
              BACS → accounts repay by bank transfer and record it.
              rma.resolved email with the credit note.
```

- **Never an account balance.** A consumer has no company account, and the law requires the refund to go back to how they paid unless they expressly agree otherwise.
- A card refund that fails at the gateway (an expired card) stays pending and alerts accounts, who repay by bank transfer before `refund_due_on`. 05.12's `refund.fallback_to_credit` is a trade-only message.

## 14. Replacement orders (signed off 2026-10-05)

> **Status: signed off 2026-10-05**, with Q-R2 and Q-R3 decided (§14.5); the other questions approved as recommended. Review of §7.5 ("a replacement order is created at zero value, linked via `replacement_order_id`, allocating stock through the standard 04 §4.2 path") against what has to be built (ROADMAP §23A, remaining B2C item 3).

### 14.1 Verdict

**§7.5 is not enough to build from.** It decides three things: the replacement is an order, it has zero value, and it allocates stock through 04 §4.2. Everything that makes a zero-value order differ from a sale is left open, and the signed-off constraints actively get in the way. `order_lines.price_source` has no value a replacement can honestly use. `orders.payment_status` has no state for "nothing to pay". The dispatch gate releases a prepaid order only once it is `paid` (05.5 §7.2). The consumer order page would offer a 14-day cancellation on goods that were never a new sale. The gaps below need answers before a replacement can be created and shipped.

### 14.2 Gaps

| # | Gap | Proposed rule |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | **How a replacement is marked.** Nothing on `orders` says "replacement", so checkout, dispatch, invoicing and the order page cannot tell. A join to `rmas` on every hot path is the wrong answer | `orders.order_kind` (`sale`, `replacement`), §14.3 |
| R2 | **One replacement per RMA, one RMA per replacement.** `rmas.replacement_order_id` has no uniqueness, so a retried request could create two orders | Partial unique index on `rmas.replacement_order_id`; the RMA row is locked and re-checked before the insert (§14.4) |
| R3 | **Line pricing.** Invariant 4 needs a snapshot, and `price_source` allows `contract, customer, promotion, tier, base, manual`. `manual` means a rep override, which this is not | Add `replacement` to `price_source`; unit price, discounts, net, tax and gross all 0, enforced by `CHECK`. `tax_rate_bp` still snapshots the SKU's rate, for the record |
| R4 | **Traceability to the faulty line.** Without a link, a fault on the replacement can't be traced to the original order and possession date (CRA s.24 final right to reject) | `order_lines.replaces_order_line_id` → the original line; required when `price_source = 'replacement'` |
| R5 | **Cost.** Zero price but real cost. Margin on the original order is wrong unless the replacement's cost is visible | Snapshot `unit_cost_e4` / `sku_cost_id` as for any line (02 §8.3), so the cost is reported against the RMA. Never shown to the customer (invariant 9) |
| R6 | **What and how much.** §7.5 doesn't say whether the SKU can differ, or how much may be sent | Same SKU and pack as the original line (like-for-like). Quantity ≤ the RMA line's accepted faulty quantity, in whole packs. Anything else is a refund, not a replacement (Q-R1) |
| R7 | **When.** §7.5 puts it at resolution, after inspection. CRA s.23 requires a replacement "within a reasonable time and without significant inconvenience", and waiting for a parcel to come back may not meet that | Default: at resolution, after inspection. **Advance replacement** (decided Q-R2): staff only, at approval, with a reason, audited as `rma.advance_replacement`. Customers can't request one |
| R8 | **Payment state.** Zero total, but `payment_status` would say `unpaid`, which blocks dispatch and confuses reporting | New `payment_status` value `not_required`, valid only on a replacement; `payment_method` NULL. Dispatch treats `not_required` as releasable |
| R9 | **Documents.** A zero-value VAT invoice or receipt is meaningless, and a replacement under warranty is not a new supply for VAT | No invoice or receipt for a replacement. A packing note is printed as for any shipment. **Built this way pending the accountant (Q-R3):** the rule lives in one place (`InvoiceService` skips `order_kind = 'replacement'`), with a code note pointing at Q-R3, so it is a one-line change if the accountant asks for a zero-value document |
| R10 | **Delivery.** Who pays carriage, and to which address? | Carriage 0 (`shipping_net_minor = 0`, `delivery_rate_id` NULL). Delivery address copied from the original order by default and editable by staff (the customer may have moved). Collection allowed, with a slot (05.6 §7A) |
| R11 | **Checkout rules.** Minimum order value is already waived (05.6 §6.1). Credit check, spend breaks, coupons and the GB rule are not addressed | No credit hold (nothing is owed), no spend break, no coupon, no minimum. A consumer replacement keeps Rule G (GB only) |
| R12 | **Customer.** The replacement must name the same customer (`orders_customer_chk`) and appear in their order history | Copy `company_id`, `user_id`, `guest_email` from the original order. A guest gets an order link (05.15 §6.2) |
| R13 | **Consumer rights on the replacement.** It is performance of the original contract, not a new distance contract, so there is no CCR 14-day cancellation and no consumer "Cancel order" | The order page hides cancellation on a replacement. "Report a problem" on it opens a fault RMA linked through `replaces_order_line_id`. Whether a consumer may refuse an undispatched replacement and take a refund instead is Q-R4 |
| R14 | **Shortfall.** 04 §4.2 can refuse the allocation. §7.5 says "the handler sees it immediately" but not what then | The RMA stays `inspected`; the handler retries later, or records `remedy_outcome = 'failed'` with a reason, which allows a refund (§13.4). No backorder is created for a replacement |
| R15 | **RMA state.** When is the RMA `resolved`? | On replacement **creation**, as §7.5 implies (§7.5's refund fields stay 0). Delivery of the replacement is tracked on its own order |
| R16 | **Notifications.** None specified | New 05.12 key `rma.replacement_created` (to the customer: what is coming and when). Dispatch sends the usual `shipment.dispatched`. `order.confirmed` is not sent for a replacement |

### 14.3 Schema (amendment to 02 §8.2–8.3 and to `rmas`, signed off 2026-10-05; all three tables are migrated, so `ALTER`)

```sql
-- R1, R8: orders
ALTER TABLE orders ADD COLUMN order_kind text NOT NULL DEFAULT 'sale';
ALTER TABLE orders ADD CONSTRAINT orders_kind_chk
  CHECK (order_kind IN ('sale','replacement')) NOT VALID;
ALTER TABLE orders VALIDATE CONSTRAINT orders_kind_chk;

ALTER TABLE orders DROP CONSTRAINT orders_payment_status_chk;
ALTER TABLE orders ADD CONSTRAINT orders_payment_status_chk CHECK (payment_status IN
  ('unpaid','deposit_paid','paid','part_refunded','refunded','on_account','not_required')) NOT VALID;
ALTER TABLE orders VALIDATE CONSTRAINT orders_payment_status_chk;

ALTER TABLE orders ADD CONSTRAINT orders_replacement_chk CHECK (
  (order_kind = 'sale' AND payment_status <> 'not_required')
  OR (order_kind = 'replacement'
      AND payment_status = 'not_required' AND payment_method IS NULL
      AND subtotal_net_minor = 0 AND discount_net_minor = 0
      AND shipping_net_minor = 0 AND shipping_tax_minor = 0
      AND tax_minor = 0 AND total_gross_minor = 0
      AND spend_break_discount_minor = 0 AND account_credit_applied_minor = 0)
) NOT VALID;
ALTER TABLE orders VALIDATE CONSTRAINT orders_replacement_chk;

-- R3, R4: order_lines
ALTER TABLE order_lines DROP CONSTRAINT order_lines_price_source_chk;
ALTER TABLE order_lines ADD CONSTRAINT order_lines_price_source_chk CHECK (price_source IN
  ('contract','customer','promotion','tier','base','manual','replacement')) NOT VALID;
ALTER TABLE order_lines VALIDATE CONSTRAINT order_lines_price_source_chk;

ALTER TABLE order_lines ADD COLUMN replaces_order_line_id bigint REFERENCES order_lines (id);
ALTER TABLE order_lines ADD CONSTRAINT order_lines_replacement_chk CHECK (
  (price_source <> 'replacement' AND replaces_order_line_id IS NULL)
  OR (price_source = 'replacement' AND replaces_order_line_id IS NOT NULL
      AND unit_price_net_e4 = 0 AND line_discount_minor = 0 AND line_spend_discount_minor = 0
      AND line_net_minor = 0 AND line_tax_minor = 0 AND line_gross_minor = 0)
) NOT VALID;
ALTER TABLE order_lines VALIDATE CONSTRAINT order_lines_replacement_chk;

CREATE INDEX order_lines_replaces_idx ON order_lines (replaces_order_line_id)
  WHERE replaces_order_line_id IS NOT NULL;

-- R2: rmas
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX rmas_replacement_order_uq ON rmas (replacement_order_id)
  WHERE replacement_order_id IS NOT NULL;
ALTER TABLE rmas ADD CONSTRAINT rmas_replacement_chk CHECK (
  replacement_order_id IS NULL OR resolution_type = 'replacement') NOT VALID;
ALTER TABLE rmas VALIDATE CONSTRAINT rmas_replacement_chk;
```

- `orders_replacement_chk` makes a priced replacement, or a sale with nothing to pay, impossible to persist. `order_lines_replacement_chk` does the same per line, and requires the link to the faulty line.
- What a `CHECK` can't express is enforced by the service and asserted by tests: same SKU and pack as `replaces_order_line_id`, quantity ≤ the RMA line's accepted quantity, and the replacement order's customer equal to the original's.
- `order_lines_replaces_idx` serves "has this line already been replaced" and the warranty chain. It is partial, so ordinary lines cost nothing.
- Every existing row is a `sale` with a non-`not_required` status and no `replacement` lines, so every `VALIDATE` succeeds.

### 14.4 Creating a replacement — the transaction

```
BEGIN;
  1. rmas FOR UPDATE; refuse unless status allows it (inspected, or approved for an
     advance replacement) and replacement_order_id IS NULL
  2. INSERT orders (order_kind 'replacement', the original's customer, zero totals,
     payment_status 'not_required', address copied or edited)
  3. INSERT order_lines (price_source 'replacement', replaces_order_line_id, cost snapshot)
  4. Allocate through 04 §4.2: collection_slots (if collection) → stock_levels, in the
     global order; a shortfall rolls the whole transaction back
  5. order_number from number_sequences, taken last (02 §11.3)
  6. rmas.replacement_order_id, resolution_type 'replacement', status 'resolved'
COMMIT;
AFTER COMMIT: rma.replacement_created; the order enters the warehouse queue
```

No `companies` lock: nothing is held on credit. The RMA row is the first lock, taken before the global order (02 §11.1) begins. No path locks stock and then an RMA.

### 14.5A As built (2026-10-05)

- `ReplacementOrders` creates the order; `ReturnResolution` calls it when a return is settled as a replacement, and reuses an advance replacement. Staff send an advance replacement through `POST /api/v1/warehouse/returns/{id}/advance-replacement` (RmaPolicy `resolve`: admin, accounts), audited as `rma.advance_replacement` with the reason.
- **Consumer returns only for now:** trade returns are settled under §7.5, which is not built yet; the service is customer-agnostic and will serve it.
- **A failed replacement later refunded keeps `resolution_type = 'replacement'`.** `rmas_replacement_chk` ties a replacement order to that type, so the refund of a failed or refused replacement (§13.4) is recorded through its credit note, refund fields and the remedy decision log, not by changing the type.
- Quantities replaced: those accepted at inspection (restocked, quarantined or written off); for an advance replacement, the quantity requested. Whole packs only, otherwise settle by refund.
- Serial-tracked SKUs are refused (as at checkout); staff create those by hand.

### 15.1 Eligibility and fee decisions

Server computes eligibility per dispatched order line, not from a client-supplied
price or returnable quantity. Cumulative requested/received/resolved returns cannot
exceed dispatched quantity net of existing active/settled returns. Undispatched
cancellation uses 05.10, not a return. **Recall first:** authorised recall overrides
window/is_refundable limits but never ownership/quantity/trace checks; this resolves
§6.1's contradictory first-failure ordering. Otherwise default 14 calendar days from
relevant dispatch, product refundable flag, permitted reason and proof follow §6.
For split shipments test eligibility against the actual returned shipment quantities.
A request uses one return reason; mixed fee categories require separate requests.

Fee snapshots use company→location→global configuration at request, never the live
rate at settlement. Existing fee-bearing reasons retain proposed 20% (2000 bp) with
£25 minimum **per line**; exempt reasons in §6.3 remain exempt. Goods/fees exclude
carriage unless separately authorised. Calculate nominal fee with integer half-up;
actual deduction = min(accepted goods net, nominal fee), so a cheap line never produces
a negative refund or new debt. Show nominal/capped deduction/refund before submission.
Waiver is accounts/admin-only with actor/reason/time, applied before settlement and
audited; customer cannot waive. Receipt may reduce accepted quantity and refund;
compute from immutable original line net/tax/discount snapshots, apportion cumulative
partial credits so the final remainder exactly closes the original amount. Never
reprice historical goods or refund the same VAT twice. Fee/refund tax uses original
tax treatment, with proposed taxable retained-fee treatment awaiting accounts sign-off.

### 15.2 Receipt and stock

Request/approval/receipt do not increase saleable stock. Warehouse verifies quantity,
serials/batches against the original dispatch and records inspection disposition.
Only accepted **restock** writes return_in movements through 04. Quarantine/write-off/
return-to-customer stay outside saleable stock and require explicit disposition/audit;
no undocumented quarantine balance is invented. Preserve per-batch/serial splits in
02 §31.5; reject foreign/duplicated serials, unexplained excess and negative quantities.
Resolve only inspected accepted quantities; rejected goods do not earn credit.

### 15.3 Settlement without double benefit

Under company/global locks, verify terminal/idempotent state, allocate gapless credit
note number, fix refund snapshots and issue one note. If the note credits multiple
shipment invoices, record allocations separately. For a £120 note against £100 unpaid
debt: ledger **+£120 credit_note, −£100 applied_to_invoice**; invoice credited_minor
+£100, credit_used −£100, spendable balance +£20. Against already-paid goods, the full
£120 becomes balance. Never both reduce debt by £120 and give £120 spendable credit.
Cash paid_minor does not absorb credit notes. A note without originating invoice can
increase balance, but accounts must choose/document that cause.

Original invoice debt is credited first up to its outstanding amount; leave excess
as spendable balance. Applying excess to a different invoice requires a separate
owner/accounts action with explicit confirmation and signed allocations; automatic
oldest-invoice sweep is deferred. On-account credit hold for undispatched portions
is unaffected by returned dispatched goods. Replays return the existing credit note;
source-keyed event and unique RMA transition prevent double settlement. Reversal is
compensating history, never deleting a note/movement. Balance payouts follow 05.2;
consumer original-method refunds cannot use this trade default.
