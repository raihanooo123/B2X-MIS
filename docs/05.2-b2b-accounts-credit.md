## 1. Purpose

Two connected subsystems that together decide **who may buy and on what terms**:

- **Part A — Account approval.** How a business becomes an approved trade customer and acquires a price tier.
- **Part B — Credit accounts.** How an approved customer buys on 30-day terms without exceeding a limit.

They are one document because they are one commercial decision. Approving an account and setting its credit terms happen in the same review, by the same person, and the second is meaningless without the first.

This module gates the pricing engine. Until a company is approved and tiered, every price resolves at `base` scope (03 §4.2 rank 5), which means the entire wholesale proposition is inert.

---

## 2. What the reference system gets wrong

Its "B2B Registration" is a second signup form at a different URL. There is no approval step, no document capture, no tier assignment, and no credit facility. A registrant becomes an ordinary customer, and negotiated pricing is handled by inviting them to telephone.

| Reference system | Consequence | Requirement here |
|---|---|---|
| Registration form with no approval | Anyone self-serves; no vetting | Reviewed application with explicit approve/reject |
| No tier assignment | Everyone pays the same | Tier granted at approval, drives all pricing |
| No VAT or company number capture | No way to verify a trade buyer | Captured, format-validated, duplicate-checked |
| No document upload | No evidence on file | Attachments with the application |
| No credit facility | Every order is prepay; large buyers go elsewhere | Payment terms, credit limit, enforcement |
| No multi-user accounts | One login per business, shared | Multiple users, roles, spend limits, approval |
| Contradictory minimum order (£500 vs £1,000) | Rule lives in prose on two pages | One configured value, enforced in code |

# PART A — Account Approval

## 4. State machine

```
                    ┌──────────── withdrawn (by applicant)
                    │
submitted ──▶ in_review ──▶ approved
                    │   │
                    │   └──▶ rejected ──▶ (re-apply after cooling period)
                    │
                    └──▶ info_requested ──▶ in_review
```

`b2b_applications.status` (02 §4.6), with `info_requested` added to the enum — see §4.5.

| State | Meaning | Applicant sees |
|---|---|---|
| `submitted` | Received, not yet picked up | "Received, under review" |
| `in_review` | A reviewer has it | "Under review" |
| `info_requested` | Reviewer needs something | The specific request, with an upload form |
| `approved` | `companies` row created or linked, tier granted | Welcome, with terms |
| `rejected` | Declined, reason recorded internally | Declined, with a contact route |
| `withdrawn` | Applicant pulled out | Confirmation |

Transitions are recorded with actor and timestamp. `rejected` and `approved` are terminal for that application; a new application is a new row, so history is preserved.

## 5. Application

### 5.1 Fields

| Field | Required | Validation |
|---|---|---|
| Company name | Yes | 2–191 chars |
| Trading name | No | |
| Company registration number | No | Format check against UK Companies House patterns (8 digits, or 2 letters + 6 digits) |
| VAT number | No | UK format `GB` + 9 or 12 digits; checksum validated; **duplicate-checked against `companies.vat_number`** |
| Business type | Yes | Closed list: independent retailer, convenience, market trader, catering, online, other |
| Contact name, email, phone | Yes | Email format and deliverability; phone normalised |
| Trading address | Yes | Postcode validated; delivery zone resolved (02 §4.5) |
| Estimated monthly spend | No | Informs tier suggestion, never binding |
| Requested tier | No | Presented as interest, not entitlement |
| Supporting documents | No | `attachments`, polymorphic on the application (02 §4.6) |

VAT and company numbers are optional because genuine trade customers — market traders, new businesses — often have neither. Making them mandatory excludes real buyers. They are strong evidence when present, not a gate.

### 5.2 Duplicate detection

Before creating the application, check for:

- Existing `companies.vat_number` match → surfaced to the reviewer, not to the applicant (it may be a second location, or a competitor fishing)
- Existing `companies.name` fuzzy match
- An open application on the same `contact_email` (`b2b_applications_email_idx`) → block with "you already have an application in progress". *Amended 2026-09-28:* on the public registration form that message goes **by email to the address only** (`application.already_open`), and the screen gives the normal confirmation, so the form never reveals which addresses have applications (05.13 §5.1). A signed-in applicant is told on screen

Duplicates are **flagged for the reviewer, never auto-rejected.** A business legitimately has several trading entities.

### 5.3 What an unapproved visitor sees

A decision, because it is a commercial one.

**Specified behaviour:** prices are visible at `base` scope with a persistent notice — *"Trade prices shown after approval"* — and checkout is blocked pending approval.

Rationale: hiding prices entirely kills discovery and is the most common reason a wholesale site converts badly. Showing base prices sets a ceiling the customer will only ever see improve. The alternative (hide all prices) is a configuration flag, not a rebuild, and is recorded as an open question in §15 (Q1).

### 5.4 Review queue

Sorted by `submitted_at` ascending via `b2b_applications_queue_idx`. Each item shows every captured field, attachments inline, duplicate flags, and the resolved delivery zone.

Reviewer actions: **approve** (with tier, terms, credit limit) · **reject** (with internal reason and an optional applicant-facing message) · **request information** (with a specific ask) · **reassign**.

### 5.5 `info_requested`

Added to the `b2b_applications.status` enum. A reviewer states what is needed; the applicant receives a tokenised link to a form that accepts a response and attachments, returning the application to `in_review`.

Without this state a reviewer's only options are approve on incomplete evidence or reject a viable customer. Both are wrong, and the second is how businesses lose accounts they wanted.

### 5.6 Approval

One transaction:

```
1. Create companies row (or link to an existing one if this is an additional
   site for a company already on file)
2. Set price_tier_id, payment_terms, credit_limit_minor
3. Generate account_code from number_sequences (02 §11.3)
4. Create or link the users row; insert company_users with role = 'owner'
5. Copy the application address into addresses
6. Set applications.status = 'approved', granted_tier_id, reviewer, reviewed_at
COMMIT
AFTER COMMIT: welcome email with account code and terms; flush the
              pricing cache key pricing:lists:{company_id} (03 §9)
```

The cache flush matters: a newly tiered company whose cached candidate list is stale sees base prices after approval, which reads as the approval not having worked.

### 5.7 Rejection and re-application

Internal reason (mandatory) and applicant-facing message (optional, defaulting to a neutral decline). Re-application is permitted after a configurable cooling period (default 90 days), or immediately if a reviewer marks the rejection as `remediable`.

# PART B — Credit Accounts

## 6. Model

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Credit limit** | Maximum outstanding exposure, `companies.credit_limit_minor` |
| **Credit used** | Invoiced and unpaid |
| **Credit held** | Confirmed orders not yet invoiced |
| **Credit available** | `limit − used − held` |

The structure deliberately mirrors inventory (04 §2.3): **an order holds credit the way it allocates stock, and an invoice consumes it the way a dispatch consumes stock.** Same reasoning — the exposure is real from the moment the order is confirmed, and a system that only counts invoiced debt will let a customer place £40,000 of orders against a £10,000 limit before a single invoice exists.

## 7. Schema

### 7.1 `credit_holds` — new table

```sql
CREATE TABLE credit_holds (
  id           bigint GENERATED ALWAYS AS IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY,
  company_id   bigint      NOT NULL REFERENCES companies (id),
  order_id     bigint      NOT NULL REFERENCES orders (id),
  amount_minor bigint      NOT NULL,
  status       text        NOT NULL DEFAULT 'held',
  held_at      timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
  released_at  timestamptz,
  invoice_id   bigint      REFERENCES invoices (id),

  CONSTRAINT credit_holds_order_uq UNIQUE (order_id),
  CONSTRAINT credit_holds_status_chk
    CHECK (status IN ('held','invoiced','released')),
  CONSTRAINT credit_holds_amount_chk CHECK (amount_minor > 0)
);

-- partial: the credit check only ever sums live holds
CREATE INDEX credit_holds_company_held_idx
  ON credit_holds (company_id) INCLUDE (amount_minor) WHERE status = 'held';
CREATE INDEX credit_holds_reaper_idx
  ON credit_holds (held_at) WHERE status = 'held';
CREATE INDEX credit_holds_invoice_idx
  ON credit_holds (invoice_id) WHERE invoice_id IS NOT NULL;
```

- `credit_holds_order_uq` makes the hold idempotent. A retried checkout cannot double-hold credit — the same guarantee `stock_allocations_identity_uq` gives stock (02 §7.4).
- `credit_holds_company_held_idx (company_id, status, amount_minor)` is covering for the sum used in every credit check.
- `credit_holds_reaper_idx` drives the reaper that releases holds on abandoned orders, mirroring the stale-allocation reaper (04 §9).

### 7.2 ⚠️ Amendment to Doc 02 §4.3

`companies` requires one additional column:

```sql
ALTER TABLE companies
  ADD COLUMN credit_held_minor BIGINT NOT NULL DEFAULT 0 AFTER credit_used_minor,
  ADD CONSTRAINT chk_companies_held CHECK (credit_held_minor >= 0);
```

Both `credit_used_minor` and `credit_held_minor` are **projections**, maintained transactionally and rebuildable:

```sql
-- credit_used: invoiced and unpaid
SELECT COALESCE(SUM(i.total_gross_minor - i.paid_minor), 0)
FROM   invoices i
WHERE  i.company_id = ? AND i.status IN ('issued','part_paid','overdue');

-- credit_held: confirmed, not yet invoiced
SELECT COALESCE(SUM(amount_minor), 0)
FROM   credit_holds
WHERE  company_id = ? AND status = 'held';
```

Per 04 §9, reconciliation runs hourly, drift is a P1, and **drift is never auto-corrected**.

## 8. The credit check

### 8.1 At checkout

```
available = credit_limit_minor − credit_used_minor − credit_held_minor
```

| Condition | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `order_total <= available` | Proceed. Hold placed |
| `order_total > available`, `payment_terms = 'prepay'` | Card or BACS required. No hold |
| `order_total > available`, terms account | Order held at `awaiting_approval`; accounts notified with the shortfall. Buyer offered prepay for this order instead |
| Company `status = 'suspended'` | On-account checkout blocked entirely. Prepay permitted unless suspension reason forbids it |
| Any invoice overdue beyond the grace period | On-account blocked; explicit message naming the overdue invoice |

Available credit is shown **before** the buyer reaches payment, on the order pad footer and in the basket. Discovering a credit block at the payment step wastes the buyer's time and generates a phone call.

### 8.2 Lock ordering — extends 04 §4.3

A single checkout transaction now locks **two kinds of row**: the `companies` row for the credit check and `stock_levels` rows for allocation. Two lock types means a new deadlock surface: two orders from the same company for the same SKUs, one taking credit first and the other stock first, deadlock.

**The rule, extending 04 §4.3 into a single global lock order:**

```
1. companies          (credit, one row, FOR UPDATE)
2. stock_levels       (ascending sku_id, location_id, batch_id)
3. credit_holds / stock_allocations inserts
```

**Credit is always locked first.** It is one row, it is the cheapest check, and failing it avoids taking any stock locks at all — so a rejected order holds nothing. Asserted by the same generated-SQL inspection test as 04 §4.3.

Everything else from 04 §4.4 continues to apply: no external calls inside the transaction, side effects queued after commit, bounded retry on deadlock.

### 8.3 Hold lifecycle

| Event | Effect |
|---|---|
| Order confirmed on account | `credit_holds` row at `held`; `credit_held_minor` += amount |
| Order invoiced | Hold → `invoiced`; `credit_held_minor` −= amount; `credit_used_minor` += invoice total |
| Order cancelled | Hold → `released`; `credit_held_minor` −= amount |
| Order total changes before invoicing | Hold amount updated within a credit re-check; an increase can fail and block the amendment |
| Invoice paid | `credit_used_minor` −= payment |
| Credit note issued | `credit_used_minor` −= credit note total |
| Abandoned unpaid order reaped | Hold → `released` |

Every one of these runs inside the transaction that causes it. None is a scheduled catch-up.

## 9. Terms, ageing and suspension

- `payment_terms` — `prepay`, `net7`, `net14`, `net30`, `net60`. Drives `invoices.due_at` at issue.
- Overdue means past `due_at` plus a configurable grace period (default 3 days).
- Aged debt buckets — current, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+ — reported for accounts, driven by `invoices_company_issued_idx`.
- **Automatic suspension** at a configurable overdue threshold (default: any invoice 30 days past due, or total overdue above a configured amount). Suspension sets `companies.status = 'suspended'`, blocks on-account checkout, and notifies the customer and their rep. It never cancels existing orders — undelivered goods already allocated stay allocated pending a human decision.
- Reinstatement is manual, with actor and reason recorded.

## 10. Multi-user accounts

Built on `company_users` (02 §4.4), which already carries `role`, `order_limit_minor` and `requires_approval`.

| Role | Capability |
|---|---|
| `owner` | Order, manage users and limits, see all orders and invoices |
| `buyer` | Order within their limit, see own orders |
| `approver` | Order, plus approve others' orders above their limits |
| `viewer` | Read-only: catalogue, prices, order history. No ordering |

**Approval flow.** An order exceeding the buyer's `order_limit_minor`, or placed by a user with `requires_approval = 1`, enters `orders.status = 'awaiting_approval'`.

The important detail: **stock and credit are held while awaiting approval.** Otherwise the approver approves an order whose stock has since been sold, and the approval means nothing. The stale-allocation reaper applies on a longer window for this state (default 48 h rather than 2 h), because a human has to act.

Approval notifies the approver immediately and daily until actioned. Rejection releases both holds and notifies the buyer with the reason.

## 12. Edge cases

| Case | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Two orders placed simultaneously, together exceeding the limit | Serialised by the `companies` row lock. The first succeeds, the second fails the check cleanly |
| Credit limit reduced below current exposure | Permitted. No new on-account orders until exposure falls. Existing orders unaffected |
| Order amended upward after the hold | Credit re-checked; failure blocks the amendment rather than silently exceeding the limit |
| Partial payment of an invoice | `credit_used_minor` reduced by the amount paid; invoice moves to `part_paid` |
| Credit note exceeding outstanding debt | Company carries a credit balance; next invoice offsets it |
| Company approved, then a second application arrives for the same VAT number | Flagged; reviewer links it as an additional site or rejects |
| Applicant approved while an anonymous cart exists | Cart re-priced at the new tier on next load, with the change shown, never silently |
| User removed from a company with open orders | Orders remain, attributed to the user record; `company_users` row removed |
| Suspended company's rep places an order on their behalf | Blocked on the same rules. A rep cannot bypass credit control |

## 13. Test matrix

**Concurrency** — the tests that justify §8.2:

| # | Scenario | Assertion |
|---|---|---|
| CR1 | 20 parallel orders of £600 against a £10,000 limit | Exactly 16 succeed, 4 fail cleanly, `credit_held_minor` exactly £9,600 |
| CR2 | Order and credit-limit reduction racing | Serialised; no negative available credit; no partial hold |
| CR3 | Same checkout replayed twice | `credit_holds_order_uq` prevents a double hold |
| CR4 | Two orders, same company, same SKUs, opposite arrival order | Zero deadlocks reported to callers |
| CR5 | Process killed between credit hold and stock allocation | Neither persists; projections match sources on restart |

**Property tests:** `credit_held_minor` = Σ held holds, always · `credit_used_minor` = Σ unpaid invoice balances, always · available credit never negative · Σ hold transitions conserve the total.

**Workflow fixtures:** application → info requested → approved with tier, prices change immediately · application → rejected → re-apply after cooling period · duplicate VAT flagged not auto-rejected · approval creating a second site on an existing company · buyer over limit → approver approves → holds survived the wait · overdue invoice triggers suspension → on-account blocked, prepay allowed · credit note offsetting exposure · hold released by the reaper on an abandoned order.

## 14. Acceptance criteria

1. A complete application can be submitted, reviewed, have information requested, and be approved, with every transition recorded with actor and timestamp.
2. On approval the customer's tier prices are live on the next page load, with no cache staleness.
3. CR1 passes with exact credit figures, repeated 100 times, zero over-limit approvals.
4. CR4 reports zero deadlocks to callers, with the lock order asserted by inspecting generated SQL.
5. Available credit is visible in the basket and order pad footer before the payment step.
6. Credit projections rebuild exactly from invoices, payments, credit notes and holds on a 10,000-order dataset.
7. An order awaiting approval retains both its stock allocation and its credit hold for the full approval window.
8. Overdue thresholds suspend on-account purchasing automatically, without cancelling existing orders.
9. A rep cannot place an on-account order that the customer could not place themselves.
10. Duplicate VAT numbers are surfaced to reviewers and never block an applicant automatically.

## 16. Implementation status — Part A review (2026-09-27, pending verification)

**Built:** staff review of applications in the admin panel (Customers → Trade applications): the §5.4 queue (open applications, oldest first), a detail page with every captured field, the applicant's account state, the trading address with its resolved delivery zone, §5.2 duplicate flags and any documents, and one action per §4 edge. `App\Domain\Accounts\ApplicationReviewService`, `B2bApplicationPolicy`, `TradeApplicationResource`.

| Edge | Action | Who |
|---|---|---|
| `submitted` → `in_review` | Start review — the reviewer is recorded | Active reviewer |
| `in_review` → `info_requested` | Request information — the ask is stored in `info_request` and emailed | Active reviewer |
| `info_requested` → `in_review` | Resume review | Active reviewer, until the applicant's response form (§5.5) exists |
| `in_review` → `approved` | Approve with tier, terms and credit limit (§5.6) | Active reviewer; a credit limit above zero also needs `grantCredit` |
| `in_review` → `rejected` | Reject with the mandatory internal reason (`review_note`) and the remediable choice (§5.7) | Active reviewer |

Every other transition is refused. **Reviewers are users holding the `admin` or `accounts` role** (decided 2026-09-27, resolving the role part of 05.12 Q6). **Only `admin` and `accounts` may grant a credit limit above zero** — a separate policy ability (`B2bApplicationPolicy::grantCredit`), re-checked under lock, so it still holds if review is ever opened to another role. No one can review their own application.

**Decisions taken at implementation, for review:**

- **Approval prerequisites.** The applicant's account must be `active` with a confirmed email (the `application.submitted` email already tells applicants review waits for this), and the trading address must be complete. A VAT number already on a company refuses the approval: `companies_vat_uq` would reject it anyway, and linking a second site (§5.6 step 1, §12) is not built.
- **§5.6 as built.** One transaction: company created `approved`, with `approved_at` and `approved_by_user_id`; the applicant's existing account linked as `owner` and default contact (05.13 §5.1, "link, not create"); the application address copied as the default `both` address, with its delivery zone; the application set `approved` with `company_id`, `granted_tier_id`, reviewer and `reviewed_at`. `account_code` comes from the `number_sequences` series `account_code` — format `ACC-000001` (prefix `ACC-`, padding 6; confirmed 2026-09-27), seeded by migration `2026_10_13_090100` — taken after every check. After commit: `application.approved` to the applicant, and `pricing:lists:{company_id}` forgotten through `App\Domain\Pricing\PricingCache`. Nothing reads that key yet, since the resolvers query directly, so the flush is in place for when the 03 §9 cache is built.
- **Concurrency.** Every transition locks the `admin` row of `roles` (StaffRoleService takes that lock for a change to any role, `accounts` included), then the application row, then the applicant's `users` row, and re-checks the policy against the locked application. Two reviewers deciding one application serialise, and the second is refused. An approval and a suspension of the applicant serialise on the user row, so a new company never starts without an active owner (05.13 §9).
- **Audit** (07 §6.5; family `permission`, since review decides who buys on trade terms): `application.review_started`, `application.info_requested`, `application.review_resumed` and `application.rejected` record only `status` before and after, plus `remediable` on a rejection. `application.approved` adds `company_id`, `owner_user_id`, `price_tier_id`, `payment_terms` and `credit_limit_minor`. An approval with a credit limit also writes `credit_limit.changed` (family `credit_limit`, subject the company, `credit_limit_minor` before and after). The internal reason and the information request are never copied into the audit log.
- **Notifications** (05.12 §5.2): `application.info_requested`, `application.approved` and `application.rejected`, each sent after commit. The information request is emailed word for word and asks the applicant to reply. 05.12's tokenised link waits for the response form. A rejection uses neutral wording, never the internal reason, and gives the re-application date — the cooling period, 90 days, or "whenever circumstances change" if remediable.

**Not persisted — needs a 02 §4.6 amendment before it can be** (signed off 2026-09-28 as 02 §25.2, see §17):

- Whether a rejection was `remediable`. It is chosen, audited and reflected in the email, but no column holds it, so the re-application gate (05.13 §7) cannot read it yet.
- The optional applicant-facing rejection message (§5.7). Not offered, because it could not be stored; the neutral default is always sent.
- The cooling period is a code constant (90 days). §5.7 calls it configurable, but no `system_configurations` key is named.

**Not built:** reassignment (§5.4); withdrawal by the applicant; the applicant's response form, tokenised link and status page (§5.5, 05.13 §7); the re-application gate; staff-entered applications with no applicant account (`applicant_user_id` NULL — listed, but not reviewable); linking an approval to an existing company as an additional site; document download (documents are listed by name only); the reviewers' copy of `application.submitted` (05.12 Q6).

### 17.1 At application

The form (`RegisterTradeRequest`, and the signed-in form of 05.13 §5.1 when built) gains:

| Field | Required | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Legal form | Yes | Sole trader, partnership, limited company, LLP or other (02 §25.3). Separate from business type, which stays the trade sector |
| Companies House number | **Yes for a limited company or LLP**; otherwise as §5.1 | Same format check as §5.1 |
| VAT number | No | `GB` **or Northern Ireland `XI`** + 9 or 12 digits, with the same checksum (supersedes §5.1's GB-only format) |
| Terms of trade | Yes | The current trade terms are shown, and their version is submitted with the form. Acceptance records the version, time, IP and browser (02 §25.1). If the terms changed while the form was open, the applicant is asked to read the new version |

After the application commits, a queued job checks the VAT number — with HMRC for `GB`, with the EU's VIES for `XI` — when one was given, and the company number with Companies House when one was given. **Nothing about either check affects submission.** The applicant sees the same confirmation whatever the outcome, and is never told a check failed or could not run.

### 17.2 What each legal form needs

| Legal form | Companies House check | VAT check |
|---|---|---|
| Sole trader | Not applicable | If a VAT number is given |
| Partnership | If a number is given — Scottish (`SL`), limited (`LP`) and NI limited (`NL`) partnerships are registered | If a VAT number is given |
| Limited company, LLP | Required: `not_found`, or an ended or insolvent status, **refuses approval** (see below); anything else short of `found` + `active` with a matching type needs acknowledgement | If a VAT number is given |
| Other | If a number is given | If a VAT number is given |

VAT stays optional for everyone (§5.1). A business under the VAT threshold is a genuine trade customer.

### 17.3 At review

- **Verification section.** It shows the latest HMRC and Companies House results and their history: outcome, registered name and address, HMRC's consultation number, company status and type, incorporation date and when each check ran.
  - The reviewer compares the registered name and address with what the applicant entered. Differences are shown side by side, never decided automatically.
  - The queue shows a badge per application: verified, needs attention, or unchecked.
- **HMRC or Companies House unreachable.** The result is recorded as `unchecked`, with the reason. **Re-run checks** (reviewers, open applications) runs both again and records who asked. Submission never waits on either service, and review is never blocked by an outage.
- **Refusal.** Approval of a limited company or LLP is refused outright when Companies House reports it `not_found`, or with any of these statuses:
  - ended: `dissolved`, `removed`, `closed`, `converted-closed`;
  - insolvent: `liquidation`, `administration`, `receivership`, `insolvency-proceedings`.
  
  No acknowledgement overrides this; the reviewer can re-run the check, or reject. A company in a voluntary arrangement (CVA), or with a status Companies House adds later, is not refused: it needs acknowledgement.
- **Warnings and acknowledgement.** Approval with any other shortfall needs a ticked acknowledgement:
  - a given VAT number `not_found`;
  - any check `unchecked` or never run;
  - evidence older than 30 days (`applications.verification_max_age_days`);
  - a company status other than `active` that does not refuse (`voluntary-arrangement`, or one not yet known);
  - a company type that does not match the legal form.
  
  The warning codes and the acknowledgement are recorded in the `application.approved` audit row. The full list is in 02 §25.9.
- **Rejection** records a category from a closed list — not a trade business, business not verified, identity not verified, duplicate account, no response to request, outside trading area, credit or risk concern, other — as well as whether the rejection is remediable, and an optional message to the applicant alongside the internal reason. The category is added to the `application.rejected` audit row; the message and the internal reason never are.
  - The rejection email uses the message when given, and the neutral default otherwise. The internal reason is never sent.
  - The re-application date is fixed at rejection: the cooling period from `applications.reapply_cooling_days` (90 by default), or none if remediable. A later change to the setting does not move it.
- **Approval** copies the legal form to the company.

### 17.4 Terms of trade versions

Administrators publish terms versions in the admin panel (Settings → Terms): kind, version label, effective date (now or later) and the text, with a preview. Publishing is confirmed and audited. A published version can never be edited or deleted; a correction is a new version. The storefront shows the version in force. Each application links to the version its applicant accepted, so a dispute can be answered with the exact text.

**Production terms must be reviewed by a solicitor before they are published.** The system records and proves what was accepted; it cannot judge whether the terms are sound. For local development, a seeder publishes clearly marked placeholder terms so registration works, and it refuses to run anywhere but `local` (02 §25.10).

### 17.5 Out of scope, and later slices

- **Credit vetting** — trade references, credit reference agency checks, personal guarantees — belongs to Part B, and will be specified with credit-limit management. The checks here verify that the business exists as described. They do not measure its credit risk. The `credit_or_risk_concern` rejection category records a reviewer's judgement, not a vetting result.
- **Monthly re-checks of approved companies** (VAT registration, Companies House status) are decided and belong to the credit module: a customer on credit terms that dissolves is a credit risk. They are planned in ROADMAP §6.
- **Public terms-of-sale acceptance at checkout** is supported by the schema (02 §25.1), and will be built in a later checkout slice.

### 18.1 Rules and transaction boundary

- Server evaluates **every** trade checkout route, quote conversion and rep/dropship
  order through the same gates. Live user membership, company state and buyer order
  limits are rechecked under the company lock; hiding a UI action is insufficient.
- Approved + terms + no overdue debt beyond configured `credit.overdue_grace_days`
  may use on-account. Approved grace default **0 calendar days**. Due_at is the
  snapshotted invoice due instant; test London DST and exact boundary. Existing
  invoices/status do not disappear on suspension. Suspended account may prepay only
  when the reason allows it; approved default: allow ordinary debt suspension,
  deny fraud/legal/manual all-sales block. Closed/rejected/unapproved cannot use trade
  terms. Show explicit blocked reason, ageing and permitted prepay/contact action.
- Outstanding invoice amount = max(0, gross − paid − credited), excluding void.
  `credit_used_minor` rebuilds from outstanding issued trade invoices;
  `credit_held_minor` from live holds; account_balance from signed ledger. Available
  credit = limit − used − held, distinct from spendable balance. A reduced limit can
  make raw availability negative; display zero plus over-limit amount, rather than
  incorrectly asserting availability can never be negative. No new positive exposure
  is granted above the limit. Unknown/errored credit state fails closed for on-account.
- Buyer limit applies to **full order gross including VAT/carriage before balance**,
  not merely the remaining on-account amount. `requires_approval` or gross above the
  nullable limit creates a buyer_limit request. Owner/approver may decide another
  buyer's request within their company; no self-approval, no viewer/rep bypass.
  An approver's own buying is subject to their own limit/approval flag. At decision
  time the live role/limit and company gates are rechecked.
- All requests snapshot one 48-hour expires_at from first submission (UTC elapsed
  hours), not from the most recent email/edit. Order prices/quantities are immutable
  while waiting: edits cancel/release and create a new checkout. Approval at
  `now >= expires_at` loses to expiry. Request audit names decision actor/reason.
- **Approved resolution of §8.1/§10 hold ambiguity:** funded buyer approvals reserve
  stock and the remaining on-account credit for 48 hours. Shortfall requests use
  credit_exception with **no stock, collection or credit reservation** until accounts
  raises the limit or buyer elects prepay; buyer approval cannot waive company credit.
  Accounts/admin can approve the funding exception but cannot approve on behalf of a
  required buyer approver. UI clearly says "Stock not reserved" for unfunded requests.
  If both requests are needed, both must pass; funding/reservation rechecks inventory
  and slot capacity and retains the original expiry, never extends it.
- On-account exposure is gross less spendable balance applied and required cleared
  deposit. Balance is debited only once a funded order reservation can commit, under the company
  lock with a source-keyed event; unfunded exception requests do not consume balance;
  rejecting/expiring unpaid orders appends one reversal. For prepaid buyer approvals,
  defer card intent until approval: then pending_payment receives the existing short
  hold (2 hours, subject to payment spec) and Stripe flow. No 48-hour card authorisation.
- Partial invoices consume the matching part of a credit hold, adding only net new
  invoice exposure to used; invoiced funds are never released by the approval reaper.
  Applying account credit to an invoice at issue requires explicit allocation, not
  reducing paid_minor or counting balance a second time.
- Original-card payouts also verify payment belongs to the same company and cap payout
  at proven refundable source-card funds net of earlier refunds/reservations. Bank
  payouts require verified destination evidence; provider calls remain after commit.
- Hourly projection reconciliation flags drift (P1); do not silently repair history.
  Credit notes extinguish original invoice debt first and leave only excess spendable,
  using the paired ledger/allocation proposal in 05.4 §15.3.

### 18.2 Coordinated reaper

One expiry coordinator runs every minute; nightly ageing/suspension is separate.
Discover candidate ids without locking stock first; process each company's candidates
with READ COMMITTED locks **company → collection slot when present → ordered stock
levels (sku, location, batch NULLS FIRST) → payment/invoice rows → order/request/hold
rows → number keys if required**. All writers must use this auxiliary order too.
Recheck state and expiry after locking; repeated jobs or crash recovery do nothing
once terminal. In one transaction cancel the unpaid order, expire pending requests,
release only live allocations/collection booking/uninvoiced credit hold, reverse
applied balance once and append audit/outbox events. No HTTP/email/payment calls in
transaction. A concurrent decision/payment webhook observes the same lock order and
one final state; settled/invoiced orders are never expired by this job. External
payment ambiguity follows existing Stripe reconciliation, never refunds speculatively.
