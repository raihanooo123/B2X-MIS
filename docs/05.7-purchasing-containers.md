## 1. Purpose

This module answers one question the business cannot currently answer: **what did this actually cost us?**

An importer's unit cost is not the supplier's invoice price. A £0.42 grater arriving from a container also carries sea freight, import duty, port handling, customs clearance and inland haulage. Apportioned across the consignment, those can add 20–30% to the FOB price. Sell on a 25% markup over FOB and you may be selling at a loss.

Landed cost is what makes every margin figure in Doc 03 §11 real rather than decorative. Without it, `sku_costs` holds the supplier price, margin reports flatter every product, and the business discounts into negative territory with no warning.

## 5. Purchase orders

### First purchasing slice (decision recorded 2026-09-26)

The first operational Filament workflow is deliberately narrower than the full module:
`admin` and `purchasing` staff can maintain suppliers and create, edit, confirm, view,
and cancel purchase orders. Other staff have no purchasing administration access.
This slice accepts **GBP suppliers and GBP orders only**. A buyer records confirmation
in B2X **after supplier acceptance outside the system**; no email, PDF, or supplier
portal delivery is implied. Drafts do not affect stock. Confirmation raises the
NULL-batch `incoming_base_qty` projection, receipt reduces it, and cancellation
removes only the outstanding amount after open Goods-in receipts are closed.
Physical on-hand stock changes only at receipt.
No supplier or purchase-order delete action is exposed. Containers, FX, landed
costing, outbound PO delivery, and a full purchasing API remain later work.

**Outstanding view (2026-09-26):** Admin and purchasing staff have a read-only
Filament list of open PO lines where `base_qty > received_base_qty`, using the
same open statuses as §5.1. It shows remaining **base units** per line, supplier,
PO, SKU, receiving location, and the line's `expected_at` when present (otherwise
the PO's date). This is an expected date, not a supplier-confirmed delivery
promise or a customer-facing ETA. Draft, cancelled, received, and fully received
lines are excluded. No stock projection is changed by viewing the list.

`purchase_order_lines_receivable_idx` is covering for "what is still outstanding on this PO", the goods-in screen's driving query.

### 5.1 Incoming stock

`stock_levels.incoming_base_qty` (02 §7.3) is maintained from open PO lines:

```sql
SELECT COALESCE(SUM(pol.base_qty - pol.received_base_qty), 0)
FROM   purchase_order_lines pol
JOIN   purchase_orders po ON po.id = pol.purchase_order_id
WHERE  pol.sku_id = ? AND po.location_id = ?
  AND  po.status IN ('confirmed','in_production','shipped','part_received');
```

A projection like any other — maintained transactionally, rebuildable, reconciled nightly (04 §9). This is what puts an ETA on an out-of-stock product page instead of a dead end.

```sql
  incurred_on          date,
  note                 text,
  created_at           timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
  updated_at           timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),

  CONSTRAINT container_costs_type_chk CHECK (cost_type IN
    ('sea_freight','air_freight','insurance','duty','vat_deferred',
     'port_handling','customs_clearance','inland_haulage',
     'demurrage','inspection','other')),
  CONSTRAINT container_costs_fx_chk CHECK (currency = 'GBP' OR fx_rate_e4 IS NOT NULL),

```

`apportion = 0` marks a cost that must **not** enter unit cost — deferred import VAT is reclaimable and is not a cost of goods. Including it would overstate every unit cost in the container by roughly the VAT rate, which is the single most likely landed-cost error.

`demurrage` is separated from freight so that avoidable charges are visible rather than buried in an average.

## 8. Landed cost apportionment

### 8.1 Principle

Two scales, and it matters which is authoritative:

- **Line-level totals are authoritative and exact, in pence.** They reconcile to the container costs to the penny, which is what accounts and purchasing need.
- **Per-unit cost is derived, in `e4`.** It feeds `sku_costs` and therefore order-line margin snapshots (03 §11).

Per-unit `e4` gives a resolution of 0.01p. On a line of 20,000 units that leaves a residual of up to £1 against the exact line total — around 0.005% of a typical consignment. **That residual is recorded in `rounding_residual_minor`, not discarded**, so the container reconciliation balances and nobody spends an afternoon hunting a pound.

### 8.2 Algorithm

```
1. Candidate lines = all PO lines on the container
2. basis_total = Σ basis_value over candidate lines, where basis_value is:
     fob_value → line_fob_base_minor
     weight    → line_weight_g
     volume    → line_volume_cm3
     units     → base_qty
3. For each apportionable cost bucket (freight, duty, other) independently:
     for each line:
         share = round_half_up(bucket_total × basis_value / basis_total)
     remainder = bucket_total − Σ share
     assign remainder to the line with the largest basis_value
4. unit_landed_e4 = round_half_up(landed_total_minor × 100 / base_qty)
5. rounding_residual_minor = landed_total_minor
                             − round_half_up(unit_landed_e4 × base_qty / 100)
6. Write container_cost_allocations + a sku_costs row per line
```

Step 3's remainder handling is the same pattern as spend-break apportionment (03 §7A.3) and exists for the same reason: **`Σ share` must equal the bucket total exactly**, or the container will not reconcile against the freight invoice.

Buckets are apportioned **independently** because they can use different bases. Freight tracks volume or weight; duty tracks value, because duty is levied on value. Blending them into one pot and apportioning once would misallocate both.

### 8.3 Worked example

A 40ft container, three PO lines, `apportionment_basis = 'fob_value'`:

| Line | SKU | Units | Unit FOB | Line FOB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Grater | 20,000 | £0.42 | £8,400.00 |
| B | Basin | 5,000 | £1.10 | £5,500.00 |
| C | Pegs | 30,000 | £0.21 | £6,300.00 |
| | | | **Total** | **£20,200.00** |

Container costs: sea freight £3,200 · duty £1,480 · port handling and clearance £320. Deferred import VAT £4,040 is recorded with `apportion = 0` and excluded.

Freight £3,200 (320,000p) by FOB value:

| Line | Calculation | Share |
|---|---|---|
| A | 320000 × 840000 / 2020000 = 133,069.31 | **133,069p** |
| B | 320000 × 550000 / 2020000 = 87,128.71 | **87,129p** |
| C | 320000 × 630000 / 2020000 = 99,801.98 | **99,802p** |
| | Σ = 320,000p ✓ | remainder 0 |

Duty and other apportion the same way. Totals:

| Line | FOB | Freight | Duty | Other | **Landed** | Units | **Unit landed** | vs FOB |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | £8,400.00 | £1,330.69 | £615.44 | £133.07 | **£10,479.20** | 20,000 | **£0.5240** | +24.8% |
| B | £5,500.00 | £871.29 | £402.97 | £87.13 | **£6,861.39** | 5,000 | **£1.3723** | +24.8% |
| C | £6,300.00 | £998.02 | £461.58 | £99.80 | **£7,859.40** | 30,000 | **£0.2620** | +24.8% |
| | £20,200.00 | £3,200.00 | £1,480.00 | £320.00 | **£25,200.00** ✓ | | | |

**The grater costs £0.5240, not £0.42.** Selling it at £0.49 — a 17% markup on the supplier price — loses money on every unit. That is the number this entire module exists to produce, and no amount of care in the pricing engine substitutes for having it.

Per-unit residual for line A: £10,479.20 vs `£0.5240 × 20,000 = £10,480.00` → residual **−80p**, recorded.

### 8.4 Incoterm handling

| Incoterm | Freight | Duty | Insurance |
|---|---|---|---|
| `EXW`, `FOB`, `CFR` | Apportioned | Apportioned | Apportioned |
| `CIF` | Already in FOB price — **not** apportioned again | Apportioned | Already included |
| `DDP` | Already included | Already included | Already included |

Validated at cost entry: adding sea freight to a container of DDP-only POs raises a warning, because it is almost always a double-count.

### 8.5 Timing and revision

Costs arrive after the goods. Freight invoices lag by weeks; duty is known at clearance.

- On receipt, a **provisional** `sku_costs` row is written using the container's estimated costs, or FOB alone if none are known. Margin is available immediately, flagged provisional.
- When `costs_finalised_at` is set, apportionment runs definitively and writes new `sku_costs` rows, dated from the receipt.
- **Order lines already dispatched keep their snapshotted `unit_cost_e4`** (02 §8.3). They are not retrospectively restated — an invoice sent is history. A finalisation variance report shows the difference so purchasing can see how far provisional estimates were out, which is the feedback loop that makes the next estimate better.
- Re-finalisation is permitted with a reason, writing a further dated `sku_costs` row. History accumulates; nothing is overwritten.

## 9. FX and duty

### 9.1 ⚠️ Amendment to Doc 02 §6.6

`sku_costs.fx_rate_bp` is renamed **`fx_rate_e4`**. An FX rate is not a percentage, and basis points imply a rate scale it does not have. `e4` — ten-thousandths, four decimal places — is the correct and conventional precision for a currency pair: 0.7853 GBP per USD stores as `7853`.

Consistent with §2.2's rule that `_e4` carries four decimal places, and with `bp` being reserved for rates expressed as percentages.

Rules:

- The rate captured is the one **actually obtained**, from the bank or payment record, not a published mid-market rate.
- Where payment is staged (deposit plus balance at different rates), the weighted average is used and both rates retained on the PO note.
- GBP amounts are stored alongside foreign amounts, never derived at read time — otherwise the rate used becomes unknowable.

### 9.2 Duty

```sql
CREATE TABLE commodity_duty_rates (
  id             bigint GENERATED ALWAYS AS IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY,
  hs_code        text      NOT NULL,
  origin_country char(2),
  duty_rate_bp   smallint  NOT NULL,
  validity       tstzrange NOT NULL DEFAULT tstzrange(now(), NULL, '[)'),
  note           text,

  CONSTRAINT commodity_duty_rates_rate_chk CHECK (duty_rate_bp <= 10000),
  CONSTRAINT commodity_duty_rates_validity_chk CHECK (NOT isempty(validity)),
  -- one rate per commodity per origin per window; NULL origin = the general rate.
  -- origin_country is nullable, and gist EXCLUDE never fires a match between
  -- two NULLs (NULL = NULL is unknown, not true), so two "general rate" rows
  -- for the same hs_code with overlapping validity would both insert cleanly
  -- without this. COALESCE collapses NULL to a real, comparable value so the
  -- general-rate case is compared like any other. Correction 2026-09-22,
  -- same class of bug as tax_rates_no_overlap (02 §6.2) and
  -- order_spend_breaks_no_overlap (02 §6.7).
  CONSTRAINT commodity_duty_rates_no_overlap
    EXCLUDE USING gist (hs_code WITH =, COALESCE(origin_country, '') WITH =, validity WITH &&)
);

CREATE INDEX commodity_duty_rates_resolve_idx
  ON commodity_duty_rates (hs_code, origin_country)
  INCLUDE (duty_rate_bp, validity);
```

`origin_country` NULL is the general rate; a specific row covers preferential or anti-dumping rates. Resolution prefers the specific — the same most-specific-wins pattern used for configuration (02 §2.7) and discount authority (05.3 §6.3).

This **estimates** duty for planning. The authoritative figure is the cleared amount on the customs entry, entered as a `container_costs` row of type `duty`. The estimate is for forecasting; the actual is for cost.

Where `products.hs_code` is missing, duty cannot be estimated and the SKU appears in the data-quality dashboard (02 §5.4).

## 10. Reorder suggestions

> **Amended and signed off 2026-09-26.** The original text below the rule was a one-line
> formula that named inputs the signed-off schema does not hold (a SKU's supplier, a supplier
> MOQ, safety and review days). This amendment defines each input from existing columns only.
> **No new table, column or index.** Decisions recorded with the product owner, 2026-09-26.

Driven by `stock_levels_reorder_idx` (02 §7.3). Advisory only: purchasing reviews and edits
before a PO is raised. An auto-ordering system on 5,000 SKUs with seasonal demand produces
expensive mistakes.

### 10.1 Scope — one row per (SKU, location)

A (SKU, location) is considered only when at least one of its `stock_levels` rows has
`reorder_point_base_qty > 0` — exactly the rows `stock_levels_reorder_idx` holds. Setting a
reorder point is how purchasing opts a SKU in. Only `active`, stock-tracked SKUs at
`is_sellable` locations.

A batch-tracked SKU has one `stock_levels` row per batch, so figures are folded per
(SKU, location):

| Figure | Fold |
|---|---|
| `available` | `SUM(available_base_qty)` over the (SKU, location)'s rows |
| `incoming` | `SUM(incoming_base_qty)` over the same rows (§5.1) |
| `reorder_point` | `MAX(reorder_point_base_qty)` |
| `reorder_qty` | `MAX(reorder_qty_base_qty)` |

`position = available + incoming`. **Stock already on an open PO counts as cover**, so a SKU
on a confirmed PO is not suggested again before the goods arrive.

### 10.2 Supplier and lead time

A SKU's supplier is the supplier of its **most recent PO** — the latest `purchase_orders`
row, by `ordered_at DESC NULLS LAST, id DESC`, carrying a line for the SKU, with `status NOT
IN ('draft','cancelled')`. There is no preferred-supplier table; one is deferred until a SKU
genuinely needs two suppliers.

`lead_time_days` = that supplier's `lead_time_days`, else config
`purchasing.reorder.default_lead_time_days` (SKU never purchased, or the supplier has no
lead time recorded).

### 10.3 Demand

Sales are **dispatched quantities**: `shipment_lines.dispatched_base_qty` for shipments with
`status = 'dispatched'` from that location, `dispatched_at` within the last
`purchasing.reorder.sales_window_days`.

Outliers: per SKU, a shipment line whose quantity is **above** the
`purchasing.reorder.outlier_percentile` percentile (`percentile_cont`) of that SKU's lines in
the window is excluded — a single 40-pallet order does not set the reorder point for the year.

`sold` = sum of the remaining lines. `window` = `sales_window_days`.

### 10.4 Rule — integer arithmetic only

No division until the final round-up, so no fractional days and no floats:

```
suggest when   position <= reorder_point
          OR   (sold > 0 AND position * window <= sold * (lead_time_days + safety_days))

forecast     = ceil(sold * (lead_time_days + review_period_days) / window)
shortfall    = max(0, forecast - position)
suggested    = round_up_to_pack(max(reorder_qty, shortfall))
```

`round_up_to_pack` rounds up to a whole multiple of `pack_base_units`, taken from the pack on
the SKU's most recent PO line (§10.2's PO), else the SKU's default sell pack, else 1 —
suppliers do not ship part cases.

`shortfall` subtracts `position`: this is a change from the original formula, which ordered
the full forecast regardless of stock on hand and would over-order once on-order stock counts
as cover (§10.1).

The original text's "round up to the supplier's minimum order quantity" is **dropped**: no
column holds a supplier MOQ. `reorder_qty_base_qty` is the floor instead.

Each suggestion reports: SKU, location, supplier (nullable), `available`, `incoming`,
`reorder_point`, `sold`, `window`, `lead_time_days`, `cover_days` (display only —
`floor(position * window / sold)`, null when `sold = 0`), `suggested`, and which trigger fired
(`reorder_point` and/or `cover`).

### 10.5 Configuration (02 §2.7 — code default when unset)

| Key | Type | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `purchasing.reorder.sales_window_days` | `int` | 90 |
| `purchasing.reorder.outlier_percentile` | `int` | 95 |
| `purchasing.reorder.safety_days` | `int` | 7 |
| `purchasing.reorder.review_period_days` | `int` | 14 |
| `purchasing.reorder.default_lead_time_days` | `int` | 30 |

Resolved at `location` then `global` scope. Nothing is seeded; the code default applies until
an administrator sets a value.

### 10.6 Surface

Read-only, admin and purchasing roles, same policy path as the Outstanding POs view. The
`/admin/reorder-suggestions` endpoint (06) follows with the admin API controllers; it is not
part of this slice.

### 10.7 Setting reorder parameters

> **Amended and signed off 2026-09-27.** §10.1 reads the reorder point and quantity but
> nothing said who sets them or where. No new table, column or index.

**Who.** Admin and purchasing — the same roles that see §10.6. Setting them is planning, not
a stock change: no stock movement is written and no quantity column is touched.

**What.** Per (SKU, location), for `active`, stock-tracked SKUs at `is_sellable` locations:
`reorder_point_base_qty` and `reorder_qty_base_qty`, integers `>= 0`, in base units. A reorder
point of `0` opts the (SKU, location) out of §10 (it leaves `stock_levels_reorder_idx`).

**Where they are stored.** On the **NULL-batch row** `(sku, location, NULL)` — the one row per
(SKU, location) that exists for every tracking mode, since it already carries
`incoming_base_qty` (02 §23.5). If it does not exist yet it is created with every quantity at
zero, which 02 §23.5 point 9 already permits for a batch-tracked SKU. In the same transaction
the two columns are **zeroed on that (SKU, location)'s batch rows**, so §10.1's `MAX` fold reads
exactly the value set, and lowering a reorder point takes effect.

**Locking.** The (SKU, location)'s rows are locked `FOR UPDATE` in the global order
(`batch_id NULLS FIRST`, CLAUDE.md invariant 6) before writing, and nothing else is locked —
the same order allocation uses, so the two cannot deadlock.

**Rebuild.** Because these columns live on a projection, any rebuild of `stock_levels`
(04 §2.2) must update quantity columns in place and must never delete and re-insert rows, or
the settings are lost. The rebuild command (ROADMAP §4) carries this as a requirement.

**Audit.** Reorder parameters are not among 07 §6.5's audited families (they move no money,
stock or permission). `stock_levels.updated_at` records when; no audit row is written.

**Surface.** Purchasing → *Reorder settings*: every active, stock-tracked SKU at each sellable
location with its current values, filterable to those with a reorder point, and an edit action
per row. The same edit action is offered on each §10.6 suggestion row.

## 13. Edge cases

| Case | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Costs arrive after goods sold | Provisional cost used for those orders; finalisation variance reported, snapshots untouched |
| Container part-received across two dates | Apportionment on the full container manifest; `sku_costs` dated from each receipt |
| PO line cancelled after container costs entered | Line excluded from `basis_total`; apportionment re-run before finalisation |
| `basis_total` is zero (all lines zero weight, say) | Apportionment falls back to `units`, and the basis problem is reported |
| Deferred import VAT keyed as apportionable | Warning at entry; `apportion` defaults to 0 for that cost type |
| DDP container with freight entered | Warning; likely double-count |
| FX rate not captured on a foreign PO | `purchase_orders_fx_chk` blocks it |
| Missing `hs_code` | Duty not estimated; SKU flagged in the data-quality dashboard |
| Demurrage incurred | Apportioned by default but separately visible, so avoidable cost is not hidden in freight |
| Same SKU on two lines of one container at different FOB prices | Each line apportioned separately; `sku_costs` records the weighted result |
| Supplier invoice disagrees with the PO | Reconciliation screen shows the difference; PO amended with a reason before payment approval |

**Property tests:** for any container and basis, `Σ allocation shares = bucket total` exactly, per bucket · `landed_total_minor` = FOB + freight + duty + other, enforced by the generated column · `Σ landed_total_minor` across lines = container FOB + apportionable costs, exactly · `rounding_residual_minor` reconciles per-unit derivation to the line total for every line · unit landed cost is never below unit FOB when all costs are positive.

**Integration:** receipt against a PO line increments `received_base_qty` and writes a `goods_in` movement (05.5 §4) · `incoming_base_qty` projection rebuilds exactly from open PO lines · finalisation writes new `sku_costs` and leaves dispatched order-line snapshots untouched · duty resolution prefers an origin-specific row over the general rate.

## 15. Acceptance criteria

1. L1 reproduces every figure in §8.3 exactly, including the recorded residual.
2. For every container, basis and bucket, apportioned shares sum to the bucket total **exactly** — no tolerance.
3. Non-apportionable costs, deferred import VAT in particular, never enter unit cost.
4. Incoterm rules prevent freight being apportioned on CIF or DDP purchases.
5. A container's line-level landed totals reconcile to the penny against its FOB plus apportionable costs.
6. Per-unit `e4` derivation records its residual rather than discarding it, and the container reconciliation balances.
7. Provisional-then-final costing produces two dated `sku_costs` rows and leaves every dispatched order line's cost snapshot untouched.
8. `incoming_base_qty` rebuilds exactly from open PO lines, and an out-of-stock product page shows an ETA.
9. A foreign-currency PO cannot be saved without a captured FX rate.
10. Supplier performance reports produce on-time, short-shipment and quality rates from captured data with no manual input.
