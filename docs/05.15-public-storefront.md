# 05.15 — Public Storefront (B2C channel)

**Status: signed off 2026-09-28.** §3–§5 need no schema change and may be built now. §9's
amendments are **proposals only**: each is migrated after its own signed-off commit to 02
(A1–A3) or 05.6 (A4). Q1 (legal review of §7) stays open and blocks public checkout go-live.

Decisions recorded 2026-09-28 (product owner):

- **Guest checkout: yes.** A public buyer may order without an account (amends 05.13 §5.2).
- **Price display: VAT-inclusive for the public, with an "Ex VAT" switch.** Signed-in trade
  users keep their company's setting (03 §10). Closes the display half of 07 §16 Q10.

---

## 1. Purpose

The public channel was brought into scope on 2026-09-24 (01 §4, 05.13 §5.2) without a
storefront spec. This document specifies what a member of the public sees and does: the shell and
branding, browsing, the product page, guest checkout, access to a guest's order, and the
statutory consumer obligations. The storefront uses the same catalogue, pricing engine
(03 §4.2 rank 5, `base`) and stock ledger as trade. It adds no pricing or credit machinery
of its own (01 §5.3).

Trade-only surfaces (order pad, bulk entry, saved lists, credit, Team) are out of scope here
and unchanged. Trade users browse the same storefront pages at their own prices.

## 2. Principles

1. **White-label.** The platform is sold to UK wholesalers. The business's own name, logo,
   colours and legal details come from configuration (§3), never from code. The product
   credit — *"Powered by B2X MIS · by Raihan"* — is shown in the footer and can be switched
   off in configuration.
2. **The price shown is the price charged.** A consumer's line and order totals on the product
   page, cart and checkout are the figures on the invoice, to the penny (§4.3).
3. **Server-rendered and fast.** Catalogue pages are Inertia pages with server-resolved props.
   No page makes a price request per product (05.1 §9's no-N+1 rule applies).
4. **Accessible.** WCAG 2.1 AA (07 §8): stock state is never shown by colour alone, every
   control is keyboard-reachable, and touch targets are 44 px or larger.

## 3. Shell and branding

### 3.1 Configuration keys

All global `system_configurations` rows (02 §2.7), all `text` unless noted. They are edited in a
Filament **Storefront settings** page (admin only, audited as `configuration.storefront_settings_changed`
with only the changed keys). No new table. `bool` values are stored in `value_int` as 0 or 1.
Clearing a text value deletes its row, so the default applies again. Logos are PNG, JPG or WebP.
SVG is refused, because an SVG served from the store's own domain could carry script.

| Key | Use | Default when unset |
|---|---|---|
| `brand.name` | Header, page titles, emails | `config('app.name')` |
| `brand.tagline` | Home page hero line | none (hidden) |
| `brand.logo_path` | Header logo (public disk path, uploaded in the settings page) | the name as a text wordmark |
| `brand.primary_colour` | Buttons, links (a `#rrggbb` hex value; contrast checked against white ≥ 4.5:1 on save) | neutral slate |
| `brand.support_email` / `brand.support_phone` | Header help link, footer, contact page | hidden |
| `brand.show_powered_by` (`bool`) | Footer product credit | `true` |

The legal details in the footer are read from the existing `seller.*` keys
(`SellerDetails`, 02 §21.3). These are the registered name, company number, registered office
and VAT number, as the Companies Act and the Company, LLP and Business Names Regulations 2015
require on a company's website.

### 3.2 Layout

- **Header:** logo, search box (`/` focuses it), category menu (top two levels), account menu,
  and the cart with its item count. On small screens, search sits on its own row and categories
  move into a drawer.
- **Price switch:** *Inc VAT / Ex VAT* in the header for guests and public customers (§4).
- **Footer:** category links, help links (Delivery, Returns, Contact, Terms, Privacy, Cookies,
  per §8), legal details (§3.1), and the product credit.
- **Error pages** 403, 404, 419 and 500 use the shell. A 404 offers search.

## 4. Price display

### 4.1 Who sees what

| Viewer | Default | Switch |
|---|---|---|
| Logged-out visitor | Inc VAT | Yes, remembered in a first-party cookie `price_display` (`gross`/`net`, 1 year) |
| Public customer (signed in, no company) | Inc VAT | Yes, same cookie |
| Trade user | `companies.price_display_mode` (03 §10) | No (their company's setting governs) |
| Staff | Ex VAT | No |

The cookie is a preference the visitor sets themselves, so it is strictly necessary for a
service they requested (PECR reg. 6(4)(b)). It needs no consent (§8.2).

### 4.2 Guest price visibility

05.13 §14 (`catalogue.guest_price_visibility`, ⚑11) still applies. If it is `hidden`, the
public storefront shows no prices to logged-out visitors. That mode is for trade-only
deployments; a business selling to the public keeps `base_with_notice`, and the storefront shows
no "trade prices after approval" banner (§5.4).

### 4.3 Arithmetic

Stored and resolved prices stay **net** (03 §10). Gross is presentation only:

- **Unit price** (product page, listings): `gross_e4 = roundHalfUpDiv(net_e4 × (10000 + rate_bp), 10000)`,
  shown to the penny. It is labelled "each", and "per pack of N" where a pack applies.
- **Line and order totals:** VAT is computed per line exactly as the order will compute it
  (03 §6, §10), and `line_gross_minor = line_net_minor + line_tax_minor`. The gross shown on a
  line is therefore what is charged. A unit gross × quantity may differ from the line total by
  a penny. The line total is authoritative, and the cart shows it.
- The rate is the SKU's `tax_class` rate for `GB` at the current time. This is exact, not an
  estimate, because **public delivery is GB-only** (§6.1 rule G): no public order can change the rate at
  checkout.

The client mirror (`resources/js/lib/money.ts`, ROADMAP §17) implements the same integer
arithmetic. No floats.

## 5. Browsing

### 5.1 Pages

| Route | Page | Content |
|---|---|---|
| `/` | Home | Hero (brand name, tagline, search), top-level category tiles, newest products (12). Featured and promotional content arrives with the CMS (05.11) |
| `/c/{slug}` | Category | Breadcrumb (`CategoryPath`), sub-category links, product grid for the whole subtree |
| `/search?q=` | Search | Product grid, with the query echoed. An empty result offers the category tiles |
| `/p/{slug}` | Product | §5.3 |

Product grid cards show the image, name, brand, the "from" price (the lowest resolved unit price
across the product's active SKUs, before volume breaks), a stock label, and *Add to cart*
(single-SKU products) or *Choose options*.

### 5.2 Filters and sorting

Brand and in stock only. Sort by name A–Z or newest. Filters live in the query string, so a
filtered page is linkable and survives Back. 24 products per page, extended with
**Show more**: keyset pages appended in place, never `OFFSET` (02 §9 rule 8). The query uses the
same search predicates as the order pad, and `BulkPriceResolver` resolves one set of prices per
page, not one per card.

*Amended 2026-09-28 (S3).* The signed-off draft asked for numbered pages, price bands, and
price and relevance sorts. Numbered pages need `OFFSET`, which 02 §9 rule 8 bans on the
catalogue. Price sorts and bands need a stored per-product price key. Prices are resolved, never
stored (CLAUDE.md invariant 3), so they would need a projection proposed to 02 first. Relevance
ranking would break the keyset order. All of these are deferred until such a proposal is signed off.

### 5.3 Product page

Image gallery, name, brand, SKU code, price (§4), pack selector (05.1 §4.2 packs, with each
pack's base-unit quantity), the volume-break table (the public buyer also gets `base`-list breaks,
03 §5), a quantity stepper respecting MOQ and increments (05.1 §6), and a stock label. The stock
label reads (decided, §12 Q2) "In stock", "Low stock" at or below `stock_levels.reorder_point_base_qty`
(when above 0), "Available to order" (none in stock, backorder allowed), or "Out of stock".

*Amended 2026-09-28 (marketplace convention, product owner):* a figure is shown to the
public **only when it is small**. At 10 or fewer available (`StockLabels::SCARCITY_MAX`), the
label is "Only N left", as on Amazon, Temu or eBay. The prompt helps the buyer, and a figure that
small tells a competitor almost nothing. Above 10, no figure is sent. The same rule governs shortage
messages in the basket and at checkout. A public buyer who asks for more than is available is told
"Only N left" when N ≤ 10, and otherwise "We can't supply that quantity right now", with no number. A
very large quantity therefore cannot be used to read the stock position. Exact figures stay a trade
and staff view (05.1 §4.3, 06 §9.4, `StockVisibility`). The page
also shows the description, specifications (attributes), RRP where set (trade viewers only,
03 §11), related products (same category, 8), and `Product` structured data (§10).

### 5.3a Shopping aids (added 2026-09-28)

None of these needs a schema change, and none shows cost or a large stock figure.

- **Quick add** on a product card. It is offered for a single-SKU product whose default pack
  meets its MOQ, which is priced and not out of stock. Anything else links to the product page.
- **Search suggestions** as the buyer types (from 2 characters, debounced). They show up to 6
  products with image and price, from `GET /search/suggest?q=`, which uses the same search predicates
  as the grid, is rate-limited, and returns only what the grid would show.
- **Recently viewed**: the last 12 product pages, kept in the viewer's own browser (`localStorage`,
  a convenience that is never sent to the server), shown on the home page.
- **Sticky add-to-basket bar** on small screens, once the main button scrolls out of view.

### 5.4 Cart

The existing cart (06 §8) is unchanged. For a public viewer it shows gross line totals
(§4.3), an order total with the VAT included stated, delivery (05.6), and the checkout button.
The trade "sign in or apply" notice is not shown.

## 6. Guest checkout

### 6.1 Flow

1. **Contact:** email and phone. If the email belongs to an existing account, the page offers
   *"Sign in for faster checkout"* without saying that an account exists. The same neutral line is shown to
   everyone (05.13 no-enumeration rule), and continuing as a guest is always allowed.
2. **Delivery:** address (no postcode lookup at launch, §12 Q3), or collection (05.6
   slots, once §9 A4 lands; until then, public checkout offers delivery only), then the
   delivery quote.

   **Rule G — GB only.** For a public customer or a guest, the country selector offers only
   `GB`, and the checkout preview, card intent and place-order requests reject any other
   `country_code` with a 422 (`country_not_served`). The rule is enforced on the server,
   not just by the selector. Jersey, Guernsey and the Isle of Man (`JE`, `GG`, `IM`) are
   outside `GB` and are refused. A trade order is unaffected (05.6 §4.2).
3. **Payment:** card only, via Stripe Elements (07 §6.4, SAQ-A). No account credit, BACS on
   account or credit terms for guests.
4. **Terms:** the terms of sale (`terms_versions` kind `sale`) must be accepted. The page also shows
   the pre-contract information (§7.1) before the pay button: *"Pay £X.XX — order with
   obligation to pay"* (CCR 2013 reg. 14(3)).
5. **Place:** `ConsumerCheckout` as today, with `orders.user_id` and `company_id` NULL and
   `orders.guest_email` set (§9). The allocation, locking and payment path is unchanged
   (CLAUDE.md invariant 6).

Guest checkout needs no email verification, because the card payment is the commitment. A
signed-in public customer still needs a verified email (05.13 §11). This is unchanged.

### 6.2 Access to a guest's order

- The confirmation email carries a link to the order page. The link is an HMAC over the order's
  `public_id` and `guest_email`, following `EmailVerificationLink`, **not** a Laravel signed
  route (ROADMAP §0.8). It lasts 90 days, and a new one is sent from *Find my order*.
- **Find my order** (`/orders/lookup`): order number + email → the same email is re-sent.
  The page shows the same message whether or not they match, and is rate-limited like password
  reset (5 per minute per IP and per identifier).
- The order page shows status, lines, totals, delivery and tracking, the invoice PDF, and the
  cancellation route (§7.2). It never shows cost (CLAUDE.md invariant 9).

### 6.3 Creating an account afterwards

The confirmation page offers *"Save your details — set a password"*. Setting one creates a
public-customer user with that email, as registration does (05.13 §5.2). Once that email is
**verified**, every guest order with the same `guest_email` and `user_id IS NULL` is attached to the
user (`orders.user_id` set, audited as `order.claimed`). An unverified email claims nothing,
so no-one can collect another person's order history by registering their address.

## 7. Consumer rights (public customers and guests)

**Needs legal confirmation before public checkout goes live (07 §16 Q10).** The following is
the standard reading of the Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional
Charges) Regulations 2013 (CCR) and the Consumer Rights Act 2015 (CRA), specified so it can be
built and reviewed:

### 7.1 Pre-contract information (CCR Sch. 2)

Shown on checkout before the pay button, and repeated **in full in the body of the
confirmation email**. That email is the confirmation on a durable medium that CCR reg. 16
requires. A link to a web page, or to terms that could later change, does not meet reg. 16.
The email therefore carries the text itself, plus the terms of sale version accepted
(attached as a PDF once the renderer exists, ROADMAP §6). The information covers the trader's identity
and geographic address, the total price inc VAT and delivery, payment and delivery arrangements,
the 14-day cancellation right with the model cancellation form, **who pays return costs**
(§7.2), and the complaints route. It is generated from `seller.*`, `brand.*` and the terms of sale, not
hand-written per order.

### 7.2 Cancellation

- The buyer may cancel within **14 days after the day they (or a person they name, other than
  the carrier) take physical possession of the goods** (CCR reg. 30(3)). For an order
  delivered in several parts, the period runs from the last part (reg. 30(3)(b)). The start day
  depends on `orders.fulfilment_type`:

  | `fulfilment_type` | Possession day | Recorded by |
  |---|---|---|
  | `delivery` | The day of delivery | The carrier's delivered date where recorded. Until 02 has a delivered state (02 §14 note on `shipments.status`), `dispatched_at` + the zone's delivery days, **rounded in the buyer's favour** (the latest plausible day) |
  | `collection` | The day of collection | `collection_bookings.collected_at` (05.6 §5) |
  | `dropship` | Not offered to public customers | — |

  Cancellation before dispatch or collection is always allowed. It cancels the order (05.10,
  not yet written) and refunds in full.
- The order page offers *Cancel items* (the model form online) until the window closes, and
  a cancellation by any clear statement (email, phone) is equally valid (reg. 32).
- **Refund deadline** (reg. 34(4)–(6)): within 14 days after **the earlier of** (a) the day the
  goods are received back and (b) the day the buyer supplies evidence of having sent them back.
  The earlier date governs, so a return with proof of posting starts the clock at the proof,
  not when the parcel arrives. If nothing was supplied yet, or the trader offered to collect,
  the refund is due within 14 days after the day the trader is informed of the cancellation.
  The RMA records both dates, and the deadline is computed from the earlier one, with a
  staff alert 3 days before it. The same card is refunded through Stripe.
- **Delivery refund** (reg. 34(2)–(3)): the outbound delivery is refunded only **up to the least
  expensive standard delivery** the checkout offered for that order. If the buyer chose an
  upgrade (for example `courier_next_day`), they get back the standard charge, not the upgrade.
  The standard figure is snapshotted at placement (§9 A3), never re-quoted later.
  If the buyer cancels only part of the order, the outbound delivery is refunded only if
  nothing of the order is kept (a reading for the Q1 legal review).
- **Return costs** (reg. 35, including 35(5)): **the buyer pays the direct cost of returning goods for
  a change of mind. The trader pays for faulty, damaged or wrongly supplied goods** (CRA,
  §7.3), including collecting goods that cannot reasonably be posted. This is disclosed
  before ordering, or the trader bears every return cost (reg. 35(5)). It is shown at
  checkout in the pre-contract information (§7.1), in the confirmation email, and on the
  Returns & cancellations page (§8.1). **Public checkout go-live is blocked until the
  statement is on the checkout and in the email.**
- **No restocking fee** for a consumer cancellation. The 05.4 automated restocking fee applies to
  trade returns only. The only permitted deduction is for diminished value from handling
  beyond what is needed to establish the goods' nature (CCR reg. 34(9)), and staff record it with a reason
  on the RMA.
- **Exclusions:** goods sealed for health or hygiene reasons and unsealed after delivery (CCR reg.
  28(1)(e)). This is a product flag, `products.hygiene_sealed` (§9 A5, not yet proposed to 02).
  Until it exists, no product is excluded.
- This runs through the RMA module (05.4) with `reason = 'consumer_cancellation'`. The detail
  belongs in 05.4, and this section is the requirement it must meet.

### 7.3 Faulty goods (CRA)

A 30-day right to reject faulty goods for a full refund, then repair or replacement. This is
handled as an RMA (05.4) with no fee. The storefront's job is only to offer *Report a problem* on
the order page.

## 8. Legal pages and cookies

### 8.1 Pages

Terms of sale (from `terms_versions` kind `sale`, versioned), Privacy notice (UK GDPR Art.
13), Cookies, Delivery, Returns & cancellations, Contact. Their content is the business's own
and editable in admin. That is the CMS's job (05.11, not yet written). **Until 05.11 exists**, the
terms of sale come from `terms_versions`. The other pages need 05.11 and are blocked; the
footer links to them only when they exist.

### 8.2 Cookies (PECR)

At launch the storefront sets only strictly necessary cookies: session, XSRF-TOKEN, the guest cart
session, `price_display` (§4.1), and Stripe's fraud-prevention cookies on the payment step.
**No consent banner is shown**, because none is required for these. The Cookies page lists
them. Adding any analytics or marketing tag requires a consent banner first. That is a
blocking review item, not an optional one.

## 9. Schema amendments — PROPOSED, for 02 sign-off

Nothing below is migrated until 02 is amended in its own signed-off commit.

**A1. `orders.guest_email`**

```sql
ALTER TABLE orders ADD COLUMN guest_email text;
ALTER TABLE orders ADD CONSTRAINT orders_customer_chk CHECK (
  company_id IS NOT NULL OR user_id IS NOT NULL OR guest_email IS NOT NULL
);
CREATE INDEX orders_guest_claim_idx ON orders (lower(guest_email))
  WHERE user_id IS NULL AND guest_email IS NOT NULL;
```

`guest_email` is stored lower-cased and trimmed. It stays set after a claim (§6.3), so it
records how the order was placed. `orders_guest_claim_idx` serves the claim (§6.3) and *Find my order*
(§6.2). No `guest_phone` is needed, because the phone is on `order_addresses.phone`.

*Checked 2026-09-28 against 02 §19 and §21.2.* A guest's card payment still resolves to its
customer. `payments_owner_chk` (02 §19) needs `company_id` **or** `order_id`, and a guest
payment has its `order_id`, so the constraint holds unchanged. The order then names the
customer. Its receipt (02 §21.2, `company_id IS NULL`) is allocated to it as for any public
order. **But both notes name the customer as `orders.user_id`**, which a guest order does not
have. The 02 commit that adds A1 therefore also adds a dated note to 02 §19 and §21.2: *the
customer is the order's `user_id`, or, for a guest order, its `guest_email`; `orders_customer_chk`
guarantees one of them (or a company).* No payment or invoice column changes. Code that reaches
a public payment's customer through `order->user` (Stripe receipt email, receipt PDF buyer
block) must fall back to `guest_email`. This is part of S5.

**A2. `terms_acceptances.user_id` nullable for a guest's checkout**

```sql
ALTER TABLE terms_acceptances ALTER COLUMN user_id DROP NOT NULL;
ALTER TABLE terms_acceptances ADD CONSTRAINT terms_acceptances_user_chk CHECK (
  user_id IS NOT NULL OR source = 'checkout'
);
```

A guest's acceptance is identified by its `order_id`, whose `guest_email` names the buyer.

**A3. `orders.standard_shipping_net_minor` — the standard delivery charge at placement (§7.2)**

```sql
ALTER TABLE orders ADD COLUMN standard_shipping_net_minor bigint;
ALTER TABLE orders ADD CONSTRAINT orders_standard_shipping_chk CHECK (
  standard_shipping_net_minor IS NULL
  OR (standard_shipping_net_minor >= 0 AND standard_shipping_net_minor <= shipping_net_minor)
);
```

This is set at placement for every public or guest order: the net charge of the least expensive
standard method quoted for that consignment (`parcel` or `pallet`, 05.6 §5.2), and 0 for
collection. It is NULL for trade orders, which have no statutory refund. It is snapshotted like
every money-affecting figure (02 §2.7), so a later rate change cannot alter a refund.

**A4. `collection_bookings.company_id` nullable (05.6 §5)**

```sql
-- collection_bookings is not migrated yet: amend 05.6's DDL before it is.
company_id bigint REFERENCES companies (id),   -- was NOT NULL
```

05.6 was written when every buyer had a company. A public or guest collection has none, and
the booking reaches its customer through `order_id` (`UNIQUE`, `NOT NULL`), as payments do
(02 §19). Until this lands, public checkout offers delivery only (§6.1).

**A5 (later, with 05.4).** `products.hygiene_sealed boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT false` (§7.2).
Listed so it is not forgotten, but not proposed yet.

the reference system's URLs are 05.11's job (ROADMAP §21) and are needed before cut-over.
